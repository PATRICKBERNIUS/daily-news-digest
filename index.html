Cryo-EM reveals the quaternary architecture of TenpIN type III toxin-antitoxin RNP complex common to pathogenic bacteria

PLOS (Public Library of Science)

Author summary Bacteria have evolved sophisticated mechanisms to survive stressful conditions and defend themselves against invading bacteriophages. Toxin-antitoxin (TA) systems are one such defence mechanism. They consist of a toxin that can inhibit bacterial growth and an antitoxin that keeps the toxin inactive under normal conditions. In type III TA systems, the antitoxin is an RNA molecule that directly binds and neutralizes the protein toxin. Although several type III TA systems have been studied, the tenpIN family remains poorly understood in terms of its structure and assembly. Recent bioinformatic analyses have revealed that tenpIN systems are much more widespread than previously recognized, including in important antibiotic-resistant bacterial pathogens, highlighting the importance of understanding their molecular mechanisms. Here, we report the functional characterization, purification, and quaternary structure of a TenpIN ribonucleoprotein complex that is conserved across several pathogenic bacteria. The structure revealed that the TenpIN complex contains two toxin proteins (TenpN) and two antitoxin RNA (tenpI repeats) molecules. We have also identified and experimentally validated few putative active-site residues of TenpN. These findings expand our understanding of the structural and functional diversity of type III TA systems and provide a foundation for exploring their roles in bacterial defence, biotechnology, and antimicrobial research.
