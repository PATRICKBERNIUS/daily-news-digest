Global Clinical Trials Forum strengthens collaboration to advance implementation of resolution WHA75.8

World Health Organization (WHO)

Members of the Global Clinical Trials Forum – a WHO managed network – gathered at the headquarters in Geneva for the network’s first in-person meeting, bringing together WHO leadership, regional and country offices, research institutions, regulators, funders, patient representatives and other partners to take stock of progress and strengthen collective action on clinical trial ecosystems worldwide.Opening the meetingWHO Chief Scientist Dr Sylvie Briand emphasized that clinical trials are fundamental to WHO’s mission because health recommendations, policies and decisions depend on reliable evidence. She stressed that generating such evidence requires more than individual studies. It depends on skilled people, trusted institutions, appropriate governance, enabling policies, sustainable infrastructure and strong links between research, health systems and communities.Dr Briand reaffirmed WHO leadership’s commitment to the Global Clinical Trials Forum (GCTF), describing the network as central to WHO’s work to strengthen clinical trial ecosystems. She called on members to be ambitious and action-oriented and to consider not only what individual organizations can contribute, but what the network can achieve collectively.Director of the Science for Health Department Dr Meg Doherty reinforced this message, highlighting the continued importance of science, research, norms and standards to WHO’s mandate. She encouraged the Forum to build on its achievements while strengthening partnerships across countries and regions and advancing implementation of the World Health Assembly resolution (WHA75.8) on strengthening clinical trials.Remembering Dr Vaseeharan Moorthy and carrying his vision forwardThe opening session included a deeply personal tribute to the late Dr Vasee Moorthy, who played a pivotal role in developing WHO’s work on clinical trials and bringing the GCTF community together. Colleagues reflected on his leadership, mentorship and unwavering commitment to equity, collaboration and locally led research.Dr Doherty recalled his principled approach and commitment to equity, while Dr Briand highlighted his insistence that research should strengthen and empower local researchers rather than bring capacity from outside. Members shared personal memories of Vasee’s generosity, quiet leadership and long-standing commitment to strengthening research ecosystems.The reflections also underscored his lasting influence on the GCTF. Participants expressed their determination to continue the work he helped establish.From global commitments to implementationDiscussions throughout the meeting demonstrated the breadth of activity underway across WHO regions and GCTF member organizations. Regional representatives shared approaches to strengthening national clinical trial ecosystems, including regulatory and ethics capacity, institutional strengthening, research networks, streamlined ethics review and trial registration, workforce development, community and patient engagement, and support for multicountry trials.A strong recurring theme was the need to move beyond individual projects towards sustainable national research ecosystems. Participants highlighted the importance of country ownership, institutional capacity, predictable and efficient regulatory and ethics processes, sustained research sites and networks, and stronger connections between national, regional and global initiatives.WHO also presented progress in translating its global clinical trials agenda into practical tools and implementation support. This includes the ongoing development and pilot of tools, training resources and progress monitors for clinical trial capabilities. Other discussions covered equitable participation in clinical research, including underrepresented populations, ethics and governance of AI-related health research, research ethics oversight, strengthening trial registration and transparency through International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP), funder responsibilities and opportunities to strengthen locally led and multi-country research. Patient and community engagement emerged as important cross-cutting priority. Participants stressed that meaningful engagement needs to begin when research priorities and trials are being designed, rather than only during recruitment, and that greater attention is needed to ensure trust, accessibility, communication and the inclusion of underrepresented populations.A network moving towards collective actionThe meeting reflected the rapid development of the GCTF from the first global forum convened in 2023 into an active network launched in 2025. The network now brings together 52 members, including four WHO collaborating centres, with members already supporting WHO and countries in areas such as knowledge-sharing and capacity-building, local leadership in ecosystem strengthening, contextualizing WHO norms and standards, and stakeholder engagement and advocacy.As GCTF moves into its next phase, the emphasis is on connecting global standards and tools with implementation in countries and institutions. Discussions demonstrated a shared recognition that sustainable change will require coordinated action by all stakeholders including government departments, research institutions, funders, regulators, ethics bodies, and patient and community advocacy organizations.The meeting concluded with a renewed sense of collective responsibility. A commitment to build on the foundations established over recent years, translate WHO guidance into practice and use the GCTF as a platform for collaboration, shared learning and implementation. In doing so, the network reaffirmed its commitment to advancing WHA75.8 and supporting countries to develop clinical trial ecosystems that are equitable, efficient, sustainable and capable of generating high-quality evidence for better health for all.
