Removal of VirD2 from a synthetic T-DNA complex of Agrobacterium by the host DNA repair protein TDP2

PLOS (Public Library of Science)

Author summary Agrobacterium tumefaciens and related species are fascinating pathogenic bacteria that can genetically transform plants by transferring and integrating a segment of their own DNA into the genome of their host cells. This extraordinary ability has been harnessed as an essential tool for research and biotechnology. The transferred DNA (T-DNA) is exported from the bacterial cell to its host cell nucleus as a complex (named the T-complex) comprised of a single-stranded DNA segment covalently attached to the effector protein VirD2; it is thus necessary that VirD2 is cleaved from the T-DNA to allow its ligation to the genome DNA. Here, we generated fluorescently labeled artificial T-complexes and developed a system suitable for in vitro study of VirD2 removal from T-complex. Next, we demonstrated that purified TDP2 is indeed able to cleave precisely VirD2 from the imported T-DNA strand. Our findings provide new insights into the mechanism of Agrobacterium T-complex processing prior to T-DNA integration, and highlight how the activity of host plant factors can be recruited to facilitate Agrobacterium infection process.
