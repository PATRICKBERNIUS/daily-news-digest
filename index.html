Phosphorylation-dependent LEF-6 promotes nuclear export of viral RNA polymerase transcripts to ensure the expression of viral very late genes

PLOS (Public Library of Science)

Author summary Baculoviruses, including the primary pathogen of silkworms, are characterized by their ability to produce massive amounts of specific viral proteins late in the infection cycle. While this hyperexpression causes severe economic losses to the sericulture industry, it is also widely utilized in biotechnology for manufacturing valuable proteins. The viral protein LEF-6 is essential for this massive production, yet its precise role has long remained elusive. In this study, we discovered that LEF-6 functions as a critical molecular transporter rather than a classical gene activator. We found that LEF-6 binds to the messenger RNAs (mRNAs) encoding the virus’s core transcription machinery and escorts them out of the host cell nucleus. Furthermore, our results reveal that the virus hijacks the host cell’s own enzymes to modify LEF-6 through phosphorylation. This modification serves as an essential molecular switch that drives the export process. By ensuring the successful transport and subsequent production of the viral machinery, LEF-6 ultimately triggers explosive late-stage protein synthesis. Our findings provide novel insights into how large viruses manipulate host cellular systems and offer theoretical support for optimizing biotechnological expression tools.
