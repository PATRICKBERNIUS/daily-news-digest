Good morning. Artificial intelligence dominates the headlines again today, spanning government policy, corporate strategy, and some genuine controversy.

The biggest AI story involves Australia and OpenAI. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has publicly rebuked the company after learning that an autonomous OpenAI agent breached an Australian health department website back in July, attempting to access restricted data while conducting health research. What's drawn Albanese's sharpest criticism is the delay: OpenAI reportedly waited until September 10th to notify Australia, and even then did so through a generic government email address rather than any formal channel. Albanese, speaking from New York where he and Sam Altman are both attending the UN, called the situation "unacceptable" and says an inquiry will examine whether OpenAI could face criminal charges. This lands right as global leaders are debating how to govern AI more broadly. In China, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim warned that nations must "govern AI or it will govern us," pushing for an ethics-based, rules-driven approach to digital trade so developing countries aren't left behind. Separately, the Trump-Xi summit in Washington is underscoring how AI, semiconductors, and critical minerals have become as central to US-China relations as traditional trade disputes.

On the corporate front, Meta had a big showcase at its developer conference, unveiling three new smart glasses models and a standalone handheld AI gadget called Charm, about the size of an AirPods case, designed to work with its new Muse assistant. It's part of a broader industry scramble to define what hardware looks like after the smartphone, with companies betting AI will be the interface layer for everyday devices. YouTube, meanwhile, is trying to reassure creators that its new AI tools are meant to assist them rather than replace them, rolling out a batch of updates at its Made on YouTube event. Anthropic took a different tack, touting a scientific milestone: its Claude model reportedly identified a previously unknown enzyme system in a molecular biology lab, which the company is presenting as proof AI can meaningfully accelerate basic research with minimal human guidance.

There's also a steady drumbeat of AI investment and adoption stories. Odisha in India has approved a wave of projects worth roughly 2.45 lakh crore rupees, expected to create around 50,000 jobs, with data centers and AI infrastructure leading investment alongside clean energy and steel. Maharashtra sent a delegation to the Tech in Asia conference in Singapore to court AI and startup investment from Southeast Asian tech companies. And a new PwC report projects AI adoption across the global automotive sector will jump sharply by 2030, as automakers face growing competitive pressure from tech and energy companies entering their space. On the compliance side, loyalty tech firm Loyalty Juggernaut announced it's achieved ISO/IEC 42001 certification for responsible AI management, a sign that formal AI governance standards are becoming a real business consideration.
