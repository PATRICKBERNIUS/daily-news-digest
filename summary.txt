Good morning. Let's start with AI, because there's a lot moving today, especially around money and infrastructure. AMD had a standout quarter, with revenue jumping 45 percent to $13.38 billion, beating expectations, and data center revenue nearly doubling to a record $8.55 billion. The company's riding the AI infrastructure boom hard, and its guidance for next quarter came in better than Wall Street expected, which is a strong signal that chip demand isn't slowing down. Meanwhile Meta is going to the bond markets in a big way, launching its first-ever euro-denominated bond sale alongside a planned $15 billion dollar-bond offering, all to fund its AI and infrastructure buildout. It's a reminder that even the biggest tech companies are leaning on debt markets to keep pace with AI spending. On the flip side, Google is actually trying to rein in costs. Reuters is reporting that Google has quietly expanded voluntary buyout offers to employees in Canada, France, Germany, Japan, and Switzerland, part of a broader effort to manage expenses even as it pours money into AI development.

There's also a growing policy and accountability conversation around AI. With the American midterms shifting power on Capitol Hill, there's speculation that U.S. AI policy could shift toward tighter regulation as Democratic lawmakers take over key commerce committee seats, and that this could pressure the UK's Labour government to align more closely with Europe's stricter AI rules rather than staying close to the lighter-touch American approach. In Asia, ASEAN's Secretary General is pushing for regional cooperation on AI safety and ethics, and Malaysia is touting AI, semiconductors, and digital infrastructure as key areas for new investment partnerships, with over RM431 billion in approved investments already flowing in. And in healthcare specifically, the American Medical Association released a new brief tackling a thorny question: when AI makes a clinical mistake, who's liable — the doctor, the hospital, or the company that built the tool? Experts in Pittsburgh are weighing in, and it's clear regulators and the medical profession are still working out the rules here.

On the consumer and applied-AI side, Apple pushed out new developer betas for iOS and watchOS that include always-listening AI tools, an AI platform tailored for China, and a redesigned Health app, continuing its push to bake AI deeper into everyday devices. In Malaysia, ITMAX is scaling up an AI-powered traffic management system to cover more than 17,000 cameras by 2027 using Nvidia technology. And in a nice small-scale story, a Maine startup called Fortune Technology is building AI inventory tools specifically for small mom-and-pop convenience stores, one of nearly 100 companies that have received state funding for AI projects. There was also an interesting one on the research side — a new AI breakthrough reportedly solved an 80-year-old unsolved math problem about point arrangements on a plane, though as the coverage notes, extraordinary AI claims still deserve scrutiny before we take them at face value.

Switching to biotech and health, it's a lighter news day but there are some notable moves. ARTHEx Biotech, which works on RNA-based therapies for neuromuscular disorders, brought on a new Chief Medical Officer with two decades of rare-disease drug development experience. Several clinical-stage biotech firms — Xilio Therapeutics, Vir Biotechnology, and Dianthus Therapeutics — all have upcoming healthcare conference presentations or hiring announcements, signaling steady, if incremental, activity in the oncology and autoimmune disease space. On the international front, South Korea and Turkey agreed to deepen cooperation not just in nuclear energy and defense, but also biotechnology and critical minerals. And Seoul is hosting a major job fair next week bringing together around 100 companies across AI, biotech, semiconductors, and robotics to recruit young talent, a sign of how intertwined these high-tech sectors have become in workforce planning.

On the health policy side, India's Health Ministry announced plans to amend its drug manufacturing rules to align more closely with WHO quality standards, a move aimed at boosting the credibility of India's massive pharmaceutical export industry. There's also a sobering report out of Pakistan, which finds the country is prioritizing military spending over food and health programs, worsening an already serious hunger and malnutrition crisis. And on a more personal note, Hailey Bieber opened up publicly about struggling with postpartum PMDD after the birth of her son, using her platform to push for more attention to women's health research — a topic that still gets relatively little funding and study.

Finally, a quick security note: Singapore Airlines confirmed that roughly 46,000 booking records may have been exposed in a cybersecurity breach involving Amadeus, its reservation technology provider. It's part of a growing pattern of airlines getting swept up in incidents tied to shared travel-industry software, so if you've flown with them recently, it might be worth keeping an eye on your account activity.

That's the rundown for today — AI's money and policy story keeps accelerating, biotech is humming along with steady but less dramatic news, and a couple of consumer-facing security and health stories rounded things out.
