Good morning. Let's start with the story dominating markets and tech circles right now: the AI sector's rocky ride. World shares and US futures bounced back Monday as AI stocks recovered some of the ground lost in last week's sell-off, with Germany's DAX and France's CAC both climbing in early trading. But the recovery comes with real caveats. UBS CEO Sergio Ermotti warned that the Democratic sweep in the US midterms could actually slow down AI infrastructure buildout, injecting political uncertainty into what's been a runaway spending story. That same nervousness is showing up elsewhere — Alphabet is reportedly exploring a $3 billion bond sale in Hong Kong and Singapore dollars, essentially diversifying its funding sources as investors grow wary of how much tech companies are borrowing to fund AI expansion. And more broadly, analysts are flagging that US markets remain heavily dependent on tech stocks even after the AI selloff, with everyone watching upcoming earnings and the potential Anthropic IPO as the next big tests of investor confidence. Meanwhile, on the ground, infrastructure build-out continues regardless — India's National Stock Exchange is rolling out liquid cooling for its AI systems and plans to scale GPU capacity past 80 this quarter, eventually hitting 224.

In Europe, the EU is taking IBM, Atos, and Leonardo to court over a €212 million dispute tied to years of delays and cost overruns in the bloc's biometric border control system, raising bigger questions about who's accountable for Europe's critical digital infrastructure.

And if you're shopping online this holiday season, there's a practical piece out questioning how much you can actually trust AI shopping assistants from Amazon, Walmart and Google to find you the best deals.
