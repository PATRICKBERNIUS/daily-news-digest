Good morning. Let's get you caught up on today's developments, starting with some notable AI stories.

There's a landmark legal ruling out of Tokyo worth knowing about: a court has granted legal protection to human voices in an AI cloning case, after a popular Japanese anime voice actor sued to get TikTok to shut down an account using an unauthorized AI clone of his voice. This could set an important precedent as voice cloning technology becomes more widespread and more contested legally.

On the regulatory front, California continues to be the epicenter of AI policy fights. Governor Newsom signed an executive order explicitly using the term "Artificial Intelligence" rather than Trump's preferred "Super Intelligence" branding, seen as a pointed rejection of the administration's framing. More substantively, Newsom also signed the "No Robo Bosses Act," a new law requiring that humans retain final say when AI is used to fire or discipline workers. Managers can still use AI tools to help assess employees, but the law adds real transparency and accountability requirements as automated workplace management tools become more common. Together these two moves show California positioning itself as a counterweight to federal AI policy.

In the AI model wars, Alphabet is making a comeback with the launch of Gemini 4 Argon, a new model aimed at boosting performance in programming, cybersecurity, and complex professional tasks. Google's rolling it out in phases, starting with cybersecurity partners, and is working with the US government on a security review before wider commercialization, with an emphasis on strengthening safeguards against misuse.

On the business and infrastructure side, AI's economic ripple effects are showing up everywhere. Axis Bank in India is looking to roughly double its already substantial exposure to data centre financing, betting on surging demand for AI computing infrastructure, with supportive government policy helping drive that growth. Relatedly, Karnataka has rolled out a new Sustainable Data Centre Policy aimed at making the state a hub for data centres while keeping a tighter check on water and energy consumption, a sign that Indian states are starting to grapple with the environmental footprint of the AI buildout. Meanwhile, market strategists are flagging AI as both an opportunity and a risk: analysts at Macquarie and 3R Investment Management both pointed to AI capital expenditure trends and uncertainty as key variables to watch for equities in the coming quarters, alongside oil prices and tariffs.

AI is also showing up in some more unexpected places. In Brazil, researchers at SPARCBio are combining biological pest control with drones, automation and AI to support more sustainable tropical agriculture. And in Lucknow, the 400-year-old Aishbagh Ramleela tradition is being updated with AI and visual effects like holograms and LED projections, blending ancient storytelling with modern tech while keeping the live performances intact.

Now to biotech and health research, where there's a lot of clinical trial news. Eupraxia Pharmaceuticals shared encouraging 52-week data from its RESOLVE trial for EP-104GI, a treatment for eosinophilic esophagitis, with patients in the highest dose group showing durable symptom relief and nearly two-thirds of all patients across cohorts maintaining clinical remission a year after a single treatment. Egle Therapeutics presented positive Phase 1 data at a Vienna dermatology congress for EGL-003, a selective IL-2 agonist designed to calm autoimmune disease by boosting regulatory T-cells without overactivating other immune cells, and the company plans to move into a dedicated study in atopic dermatitis later this year. Unicycive Therapeutics resubmitted its new drug application for oxylanthanum carbonate, a kidney disease therapy, after adding more manufacturing and stability data. Laverock Therapeutics announced new in vivo programs spanning CAR-T cell therapy and genetic medicine, built on its gene control technology. And Gain Therapeutics will present new research on its allosteric small molecule therapies at a Parkinson's disease conference in Seoul next week.

On the financial side of biotech, Immix Biopharma closed a hundred-twenty-five million dollar stock offering backed by major US biotech institutional investors, giving it fresh capital to advance its pipeline. And Bright Minds Biosciences will be participating in upcoming conferences focused on Prader-Willi Syndrome research, continuing its work on CNS disorder treatments.

A few other smaller items worth a mention: Switzerland's President Guy Parmelin is set to make a state visit to India from October 5th to 7th, covering trade and technology cooperation and marking the first anniversary of the India-EFTA trade agreement. Xiaomi launched a new budget smartphone in India, the Redmi 17C 5G, featuring a large battery and 5G support. And the Press Information Bureau has officially debunked a viral claim circulating online about Saturdays becoming a public holiday for government banks, so that one's confirmed fake.
