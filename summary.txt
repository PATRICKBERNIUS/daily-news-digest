Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, since that's where most of today's action is concentrated. The big story is a claim out of Anthropic that one of its mathematicians, working with the company's Claude model, has solved what's being called a "Holy Grail" math problem — the second time in recent months that an AI company or someone linked to one has made this kind of claim. It's a notable milestone if it holds up, and it's fueling bigger conversations about where AI's capabilities are heading. On that note, there's also a story making the rounds about another Anthropic researcher, Tao Lin, who's warning that AI could eventually make humans permanently unemployable — a stark forecast that's getting more attention than the usual doom-and-gloom AI takes. Relatedly, The Economist is looking at how AI is already transforming academic fields, starting with mathematics and now creeping into economics, where researchers at Harvard are noticing large language models changing the very shape of papers being submitted to top journals.

On the friction side, three of the largest school districts in the US — New York City, Los Angeles, and Chicago — are considering dropping Google as their default search provider after the company refused to strip AI features out of the search tools students use, a sign that tension between big tech and public institutions over AI in education is heating up. Meanwhile, Australia's intelligence chief has raised alarms about data centres and AI creating new vulnerabilities to foreign interference and state-sponsored threats, and there's a piece examining a nationwide FAA ground stop earlier this month, which involved outdated air traffic control systems colliding badly with a new AI-assisted traffic management tool.

Economically, AI continues to be a dominant theme. JPMorgan is forecasting resilient global growth into 2027, driven substantially by corporate AI spending, even as inflation is expected to ease. But the IMF's Kristalina Georgieva struck a more cautious note, calling for tighter monetary policy as global inflation sits at 4.7%, pointing specifically to AI-driven energy demand as an inflationary pressure. In the corporate world, AI capabilities are now a major driver of mergers and acquisitions in the tech services sector, with firms increasingly snapping up companies that specialize in enterprise AI deployment. And India is being highlighted as a rising digital power, with AI, 5G expansion, and data centre investment expected to drive its next wave of economic growth.

On the lighter and more offbeat side, there's a piece using AI to "predict" the score of the upcoming Liverpool-Manchester United match, and another drawing a surprisingly thoughtful line between Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels and modern anxieties about artificial intelligence, arguing the 18th-century satirist anticipated some of today's AI pitfalls.

Switching to biotech and health, the most consequential news is out of Capricor Therapeutics, which has received a Complete Response Letter from the FDA for Deramiocel, its cell therapy for Duchenne muscular dystrophy. The FDA says the data submitted isn't sufficient to prove effectiveness, which is a real setback for patients and families who've been watching this therapy closely. There's a cluster of smaller biotech corporate updates too: Iovance Biotherapeutics and Avalo Therapeutics both announced routine inducement stock grants to new employees, and Alps Group, a Malaysia-based biotech company, confirmed it has regained compliance with Nasdaq's minimum bid price rules after a prior listing concern.

On the policy and investment side, India's government is making a big bioeconomy push, with Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh stating ambitions to grow the bioeconomy to $300 billion by 2030 and eventually $1 trillion by 2047, with biotech research institutions being consolidated to support that goal. There's also a smaller story about a STEM outreach program in Punjab exposing schoolkids to biotechnology careers, part of a broader push to build interest in science early.

Rounding out the technology and general news, Xiaomi's new Mijia Smart Air Purifier 6 is getting a strong review for balancing price and performance, timely given the usual seasonal air quality concerns in North India. There are leaks swirling around the next iPhone, the iPhone 18, expected sometime in 2027 with a new chip and display upgrades. Maruti Suzuki has nudged up car prices slightly due to cost pressures. And in India, Prime Minister Modi used a skills ceremony in Lucknow to praise ITI-trained workers, saying they're in no way inferior to IIT graduates — a message about dignity of labor. There's also a human-interest story out of Pune, where a university student was found safe after being trapped overnight in a lift with no mobile signal. Finally, Chongqing is hosting an international talent conference drawing Nobel laureates and global scientists as China pushes to position the city as a science and innovation hub, and in Karnataka, a CSR firm called SoulAce has signed an agreement with the state government to build out a digital inclusion and e-waste recycling program.

That's the rundown — plenty of AI-driven economic anxiety and ambition today, a real regulatory stumble in biotech with Capricor, and a strong thread of countries, from India to Australia to China, all jockeying for position in the AI and biotech era.
