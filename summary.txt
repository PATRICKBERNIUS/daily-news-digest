Good morning. Let's start with the biggest story of the day: Anthropic's IPO. The Claude maker raised more than 100 billion dollars, valuing the company at roughly 1.5 trillion dollars in one of the largest public offerings ever, with shares starting to trade on the Nasdaq today. But even with that eye-watering valuation, Anthropic itself is warning that it needs rapid, continued growth just to keep pace with its own spending commitments, which include more than 900 million dollars tied to data centre leases, including a planned Canadian facility. It's a reminder that even AI's biggest winners are deeply exposed to the capital intensity of this build-out.

That build-out is creating friction elsewhere too. In upstate New York, a quiet town called East Fishkill pushed back hard against a proposed data center, with residents packing a town board meeting to voice opposition — a sign that the physical footprint of AI infrastructure, the power and water-hungry server farms, is becoming a genuine local political issue in communities that never asked for them. Meanwhile, on the chip side, there's a report that the US Commerce Department rushed through export approvals for Nvidia's H200 AI chips to China, with an official reportedly pressuring staff to approve licenses over internal objections — another sign of how tangled AI hardware has become with geopolitics.

On the economic upside, South Korea just posted annual exports above 1 trillion dollars for the first time ever, driven largely by semiconductors and computers riding the AI boom. India's markets also had a strong day, with the Sensex jumping nearly 990 points and the Nifty reclaiming 25,500, led by autos, consumer durables, and banking stocks.

There's a lot of discussion today about how AI is reshaping work and business strategy. One piece argues that companies succeed with AI not by adopting it early, but by fundamentally reorganizing how they create and deliver value. More provocatively, the CEO of a startup called Arist predicts that AI agents could soon be advising executives not just on product launches but on who to lay off, raising obvious concerns about bias, fairness, and accountability in decisions that affect people's livelihoods. Former Google CEO Eric Schmidt weighed in too, arguing that AI companies have done a poor job communicating the technology's benefits, which he says is why public anxiety about AI is so high despite widespread use of tools like Gemini.

On the infrastructure and enterprise side, Acceldata hosted a major summit in Bengaluru arguing that as AI shifts from experimentation to autonomous action, companies need to move toward autonomous data management — essentially rethinking data architecture to keep pace with agentic AI systems. Bengaluru was a hub of activity generally today, with its Tech Summit showcasing fifty new startup products across AI, fintech, and biotech, and Biocon's Kiran Mazumdar-Shaw telling the crowd there that AI could meaningfully transform drug discovery and diagnostics, while also flagging that biotech still desperately needs more patient, long-term capital.

There's also a thoughtful piece questioning the usual narrative of AI leadership, arguing Africa isn't actually behind in AI — it's just being measured by the wrong yardstick, since the focus on large language models ignores real progress in computer vision and locally relevant datasets.

Switching to biotech and health research, there's a lot of movement in cancer treatment. Immutep presented encouraging data from its EFTISARC-NEO trial in soft tissue sarcoma, showing strong disease-free survival rates tied to biomarkers like tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte levels — a promising sign for its LAG-3 immunotherapy approach. Pfizer signed a hefty 2.6 billion dollar licensing deal with Starna Therapeutics for preclinical in vivo CAR-T programs targeting autoimmune diseases, continuing its push into cell therapy. In Spain, mAbxience and OncoPrecision struck a manufacturing and investment deal around an antibody-drug conjugate candidate for hard-to-treat cancers. And in a genuinely notable first, Providence Therapeutics enrolled its first patient in what's being called the world's first clinical trial of a personalized mRNA vaccine for pediatric brain cancer — a significant moment for an area of oncology that's been brutally difficult to treat. On the neurology side, Pheno Therapeutics secured two new patents protecting its GPR17 antagonist pipeline aimed at neurodegenerative disease.

Finally, a few collaboration and education notes: Bengaluru's C-CAMP signed a partnership with France's Genopole to deepen biotech research ties between India and France, India and Italy discussed new joint ventures and tech partnerships, and Manipal University Jaipur announced it's adding AI and emerging-tech electives across several of its online degree programs starting in 2027, part of a broader push by universities to align curricula with where the job market is heading.
