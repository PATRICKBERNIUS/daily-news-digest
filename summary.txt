Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, which is touching everything from elections to earthquakes in the chip market today.

In Washington, the FCC is weighing a request from a conservative group to loosen restrictions on political robocalls, including those generated with AI voices, ahead of November's midterms — a move that's already raising concerns about AI-driven disinformation heading into election season. Meanwhile, on the commercial side, Mastercard is exploring how consumers might gradually hand over purchasing authority to AI shopping agents, betting that trust builds incrementally through repeated successful transactions rather than all at once.

The AI hardware boom continues to reshape global manufacturing and real estate. Samsung Electronics posted a nearly ninefold jump in quarterly operating profit, hitting a record $80.1 billion, thanks to surging demand for AI memory chips — though the results still missed analyst expectations, a reminder that even blockbuster AI-driven earnings can disappoint sky-high market hopes. Malaysia, meanwhile, is literally building new land for this boom, reclaiming 2,300 acres of sea near Penang to create a semiconductor hub called Silicon Island. And on the workforce side, Malaysia's Ministry of Higher Education says it wants to produce 30,000 AI-skilled graduates by 2030 to meet rising demand. Asus is targeting that same AI wave with new ProArt laptops built specifically to run AI models locally rather than in the cloud.

In India, there's an interesting economic debate forming around AI costs. Zoho co-founder Sridhar Vembu says falling AI token prices are good news for India, since cheaper AI tools should actually reduce corporate spending over time even as adoption grows. Separately, the World Bank's Neelkanth Mishra argues India could unlock much bigger economic gains from AI if it opens up enterprise data access, while still letting data owners control how it's used. And HCLTech is rolling out 20 new industry-specific AI solutions spanning banking, healthcare, retail, and manufacturing, signaling that enterprise AI is moving from pilot projects into real deployment.

On the security front, AI is a double-edged sword today. South Korean authorities are racing to track down hackers behind a wave of AI-assisted cyberattacks on major banks, with one global cybersecurity firm already pointing to possible leads. And in a more dramatic story, Russia's leading AI and tech company, Yandex, says a major data center was hit by suspected Ukrainian drones this week, sparking a fire and forcing a full shutdown of operations — a reminder that AI infrastructure is now squarely a wartime target. Relatedly, a leaked EU report warns that only about a third of the bloc's counter-drone defense programs will actually be ready by 2030, despite a sixfold jump in investment this year, due to fragmented procurement and industry struggling to keep pace.

Switching to biotech and health research, there's a lot of movement globally. In India, Gujarat announced a 100-crore-rupee plan to build a Marine Biotechnology Research and Innovation Corridor in Porbandar, aiming to connect marine science, biomanufacturing, and commercialization into one ecosystem as part of its blue bioeconomy push. In Africa, Bio Usawa Biotechnology is expanding into Botswana, a move meant to strengthen regional manufacturing capacity and improve access to affordable biologic medicines across the continent.

On the clinical trial front, MAIA Biotechnology announced it's hit enrollment milestones in its lung cancer drug trials, completing enrollment in one phase and reaching 65 percent of its target in a pivotal Phase 3 study. And MUVON Therapeutics shared encouraging 12-month data showing durable results from its cell-based muscle regeneration therapy for stress urinary incontinence, a condition affecting over 150 million women worldwide with currently no approved treatment addressing its root cause — these results were presented at a major continence conference in the Netherlands. On the business side, Sudarshan Pharma pulled out of a planned stake acquisition in a US biotech firm, canceling the deal that had been announced just last month.

Finally, a few odds and ends worth a quick mention: Hong Kong stocks slipped today as rising oil prices and weak selling in tech and biotech shares weighed on sentiment, despite some optimism around Chinese policy support. Skydance's CEO is warning staff of "difficult decisions" ahead as the company integrates following its $110 billion deal with Warner Bros. Discovery. And in India, Noise launched new open-ear earbuds with Bose sound tuning, while the GST Council kicked off meetings in Delhi to discuss the next phase of tax reforms.

That's the rundown — plenty of AI investment and infrastructure news today, alongside steady progress in biotech trials and some reminders of how exposed tech infrastructure has become in conflict zones.
