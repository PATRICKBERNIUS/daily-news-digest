Good morning. Let's get you caught up on today's biggest developments, and there's a lot happening on the AI front.

Starting with the geopolitical angle, China is moving forward with new AI export curbs that are getting a lot of attention. Rather than restricting AI outright, the strategy seems designed to keep the most valuable models and technology onshore while still allowing foreign companies to access them through cloud platforms. Essentially, Beijing wants to keep its "crown jewels" at home but still profit from international demand, a business model that's increasingly favored by big players on both sides of the US-China tech rivalry.

On the corporate and investment side, the AI boom continues to ripple through hardware and infrastructure. Seagate posted blowout quarterly earnings, with net income jumping to $1.76 billion from $549 million a year ago, driven largely by surging demand for data storage tied to AI infrastructure. Similarly, Dutch semiconductor equipment maker ASM International raised its guidance and pulled forward its growth targets by three years, again citing AI-driven chip demand. And in a story that captures just how seriously investors are taking AI risk, Anthropic's IPO filing revealed some eyebrow-raising disclosures: the company flagged that its own technology could potentially threaten humanity, that it could be nationalized, or misused to create biological weapons. A related piece notes that Anthropic also quietly flagged Trump's immigration policies as a business risk, since restrictive immigration rules could make it harder for the company to attract the global AI talent it depends on.

AI is also showing up heavily in everyday business and governance. McKinsey released a report showing that agentic AI could cut the manual workload for bank transaction-monitoring investigations by 30 to 50 percent, while actually improving compliance reporting quality. Meanwhile in Malaysia, the Chief Justice announced that courts will roll out new rules within weeks governing how lawyers and court officials can use AI in legal proceedings, a sign that regulators everywhere are racing to keep pace with adoption. In India, Tally Solutions, the 40-year-old business software company, is undertaking what its managing director calls its sixth major architectural overhaul, this time rebuilding core systems like its database and memory manager specifically to integrate AI, acknowledging that the software landscape is shifting fast. And education is adapting too, Deakin University has launched a new Master of Applied Artificial Intelligence program at its GIFT City campus in India, expanding its AI and tech postgraduate offerings for Indian students.

On the talent and funding front, there's a notable startup story. Manohar Paluri, who spent 14 years at Meta and rose to VP of AI, has left to launch Warp Sciences, a startup that's already raised $215 million. The company is combining AI, robotics, and chemistry to accelerate drug discovery, a clear sign that AI's next big frontier is biotech and pharma.

Speaking of biotech, there were several developments worth flagging. A UK space biotech company, Frontier Space, has partnered with Atelerix, which specializes in hydrogel-based cell preservation, to test how biological samples can be preserved during microgravity research missions, a step toward more practical in-orbit biomanufacturing. In the clinical world, Rapport Therapeutics is raising substantial capital, launching $200 million in stock and $150 million in convertible notes to fund its neurological and psychiatric drug programs, while Cogent Biosciences will be presenting at a major healthcare innovation conference in November. And on the commercial aesthetics side, Korean biotech firm CELSET has launched SOLTOX, a new botulinum toxin brand, as it pushes into international medical aesthetics markets.

Zooming out, India had a big innovation moment today. Prime Minister Modi inaugurated the Emerging Science, Technology and Innovation Conclave in New Delhi, a three-day event bringing together experts to discuss AI, biotech, and healthcare priorities, alongside an exhibition featuring roughly 150 deep-tech startups. That ties into another report suggesting India's space economy could reach $44 billion by 2033, reinforcing the government's push to position science and tech as major economic drivers.

On the consumer tech side, Apple's foldable iPhone Duo is now available in India with Diwali discounts bringing it under 225,000 rupees, and reviewers are also praising the new Apple Watch Series 12 for meaningful health-tracking upgrades, even though it keeps the same familiar design. Meanwhile, Chinese battery giant CATL unveiled a new 9-megawatt-hour energy storage system in Australia and signed three new partnership agreements, continuing its aggressive overseas expansion into renewable energy infrastructure.

That's the roundup, a day dominated by AI's expanding footprint across finance, law, education, and biotech, alongside strong earnings tied to the AI hardware boom and major funding moves across the health and pharma world.
