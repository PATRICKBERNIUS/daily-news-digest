Good morning. Let's start with the story dominating markets this week: artificial intelligence anxiety. Wall Street took a serious hit on Thursday after sweeping Democratic victories in the midterm elections, with investors worried that a wave of newly emboldened Democratic lawmakers will crack down on the energy-hungry data centers powering the AI boom. The selloff wiped out about $2.5 trillion from US stocks in a single day, and Nvidia's Jensen Huang personally lost $15 billion, dropping him to 13th on the global billionaire list. The pain spread overseas too, with Japan's Nikkei tumbling more than 6 percent at the open before recovering some ground, and other Asian markets following suit. European bond markets reflected the same jitters, with Eurozone yields heading for their steepest weekly drop since September as investors fled to safety. Not everyone is bearish, though — Citadel strategist Scott Rubner is telling clients this correction looks more like a buying opportunity than a warning sign, pointing to how lightly positioned trend-following funds and retail investors currently are.

Tripadvisor became one of the more dramatic casualties of this broader AI unease, with shares plunging 27 percent after the company announced its CEO and CFO are both departing, compounding worries about stagnant revenue and the existential threat AI search tools pose to traditional travel review platforms. Meanwwhile, the regulatory environment for AI and data centers looks set to get tougher regardless of what happens in Washington — the midterms saw a wave of Democrats elected on promises to clamp down on data center expansion, and even if President Trump resists a federal crackdown, state and local pushback is expected to intensify.

On the enterprise AI front, India's Coforge has joined SpaceX's Super Intelligence Partner Network to help businesses integrate Elon Musk's Grok AI tools into their operations, a sign that the competition to control enterprise AI deployment keeps heating up even as public markets sour on the sector. Allstate is also pushing deeper into AI, with CEO Tom Wilson announcing a personal "agentic assistant" for customers that's been in development for years and is expected to launch in early 2027.

Not all the AI news is about dollars and cents — there's a darker side too. Federal prosecutors in New York indicted three people for allegedly using AI to impersonate corporate executives and attorneys in a scheme that stole roughly $129 million, a stark reminder of how generative AI is supercharging fraud. On a more hopeful note, a new review highlights how AI and machine learning could accelerate the development of eco-friendly marine antifouling coatings, helping ships reduce drag, fuel use, and emissions, though researchers say data scarcity and inconsistent testing standards remain hurdles. And in consumer health, a McKinsey survey found more than half of people are now using AI tools for wellness advice, even though roughly 40 percent of American users admit they're worried the guidance might be inaccurate — a tension that's also playing out around Deepinder Goyal's Temple wearable device, which is facing pushback from neurologists skeptical of its claims about tracking brain blood flow, stress, and fatigue.

Switching to biotech, there's a lot of clinical news this week. Brii Biosciences presented encouraging late-breaking data at the AASLD liver meeting in Denver, showing its BRII-179-based treatment strategy helped prime immune responses against hepatitis B, with over 40 percent of participants in one study clearing surface antigen by the end of treatment. Munich-based Ethris reported positive Phase 2a results for a nasally administered mRNA therapy designed to fight rhinovirus-triggered asthma attacks, showing a 40-fold reduction in viral load compared to placebo. HERVolution Therapeutics published translational data suggesting a retrovirus-derived protein called HERV-K could serve as a promising "off-the-shelf" target for cancer vaccines, based on findings across more than 200 lung tumor samples. And Anaheim Bio launched with 40 million euros in Series A funding, spun out of Alesta Therapeutics, to advance a treatment for a painful joint condition called calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease.

On the corporate side of biotech, Annovis Bio priced a $21 million public offering to keep funding its Alzheimer's and Parkinson's drug candidate, Lakewood-Amedex completed a preferred stock redemption, and Puma Biotechnology posted a solid quarter with $6 million in net income. Paragon Biosciences was named Overall Biotech Company of the Year at the 2026 BioTech Breakthrough Awards.

Finally, a few odds and ends worth knowing: India is ramping up preparedness against a new foot-and-mouth disease strain threatening livestock from West Asia, even though it hasn't reached the country yet. And defense contractor CSG secured a slot on a $4.2 billion US Army program to modernize ammunition manufacturing plants, building on earlier work in Iowa. That's the rundown — plenty of turbulence in tech markets, but real progress continuing quietly in the biotech labs.
