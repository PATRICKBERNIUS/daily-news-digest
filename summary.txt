Good morning. Let's get you caught up on what's moving today, starting with the big AI and tech earnings story, because it's a busy one.

Microsoft kicked things off with a strong quarter, beating expectations thanks to accelerating Azure cloud growth, with revenue up 19% year over year to $92.28 billion and net income jumping to $36.63 billion. The company also reassured investors it can keep funding its massive AI buildout without straining its balance sheet. Samsung Electronics had an even bigger headline, posting a record $80.19 billion quarterly operating profit as demand for AI memory chips surges, firing back in the AI chip race against rivals. And Marvell Technology is reportedly in advanced talks to acquire Lumentum, another sign of how the AI infrastructure boom is reshaping the chip and networking hardware space, with Marvell already a major beneficiary of demand for custom AI silicon and data center networking gear.

Not everyone's riding the wave smoothly, though. China's AI and semiconductor stocks just took a serious hit, with the STAR50 index down 40% from its July peak, wiping out roughly $480 billion in market value. Stretched valuations, doubts about actual AI demand, and fears of tighter US export restrictions are all being blamed, suggesting the AI trade in China may be cooling off even as it accelerates in the US and Korea.

On the workplace side, Amazon is telling its software engineers to fundamentally rethink their jobs as AI takes over more coding tasks. The message isn't that engineers become obsolete, but that judgment, responsibility for outcomes, and product quality matter more than ever, and the company is explicitly warning managers not to use code output or usage stats as a proxy for real productivity. Meanwhile Dropbox is cutting about 9% of its workforce, roughly 200 roles, and its CTO is stepping down as the company redirects spending toward growth areas, a reminder that AI-era restructuring is hitting even profitable companies.

In India, there's a notable government push on AI adoption, with officials identifying 762 potential AI applications across 62 ministries, covering everything from farming and railway safety to tax compliance and healthcare. Some are already live, others still in pilot stages. Mumbai is also leaning into AI creativity, hosting a summit called SPARK where VerSe Innovation showed off AI-generated filmmaking to a room of 500-plus industry leaders, and India Blockchain Week is set to bring global Web3 and crypto figures to Mumbai early next year to talk institutional blockchain adoption alongside AI.

Now to biotech, where there's a real geopolitical story developing. The US has banned imports from three Chinese biotech firms, BGI Group, BGI Genomics, and MGI Tech, over alleged forced labor links in Xinjiang. BGI denies the allegations, but this adds biotechnology to the growing list of fronts in US-China tech tension, alongside chips and AI.

On the research side, there's encouraging cancer news. Scientists are reporting preclinical success in programming macrophages, a type of immune cell, to attack solid tumors, which have historically been harder to treat than blood cancers. Separately, a company called HERVolution published data showing that engineered antigens based on ancient viral DNA embedded in our genome can train immune cells to recognize and attack cancer across dozens of cancer types, an early but promising step toward new immunotherapies.

AI is also quietly transforming biotech operations. Anthropic says its Claude model cut document review time by 89% for a drug manufacturer handling complex compliance records, a good example of AI's less flashy but very practical impact on pharma logistics. And in the startup world, funding keeps flowing fast, with more than $2.45 billion announced in the last day alone across manufacturing, AI computing, robotics, and biotech. The biggest check was $2.1 billion for Re:Build Manufacturing to expand US manufacturing capacity. Also worth noting, a former Meta AI vice president, Manohar Paluri, has left after 14 years to launch Warp Sciences, a startup combining AI, robotics, and chemistry to speed up drug discovery, and it's already raised $215 million.

A few smaller items worth a mention: Singapore Airlines has signed a deal to buy sustainable aviation fuel made from waste carbon for delivery starting in 2028, battery maker CATL unveiled a new high-capacity energy storage system in Australia alongside new partnership deals, and a Korean biotech firm called CELSET has launched a new botulinum toxin brand aimed at international markets. That's the rundown for today.
