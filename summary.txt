Let's start with the biggest geopolitical story of the day: Trump hosted Xi Jinping for a lavish state dinner at the White House, marking Xi's first visit to Washington in over a decade. The two leaders struck a notably warm tone, framing their relationship as one of friends rather than rivals, and extended their trade truce. But don't read too much into the pageantry — real differences remain over Taiwan and, notably, artificial intelligence. Xi pushed the idea that AI development needs to stay under human control, echoing global anxieties about the technology's rapid, largely unchecked advance. Analysts are cautioning that the warmth on display doesn't paper over deep mistrust between Washington and Beijing, so don't expect major breakthroughs just because the dinner went well.

On the AI business front, Amazon is quietly reaching back out to former employees — including some it laid off in recent cuts — to fill AI and machine-learning roles as it reorganizes its artificial intelligence operations. It's a telling sign of how tight the talent market for AI specialists remains, even as companies simultaneously trim headcount elsewhere. Meanwhile, AI's reach keeps expanding into unexpected corners: US mining company KoBold Metals is using AI-directed exploration to hunt for critical minerals deep in the Democratic Republic of Congo, hoping algorithmic prospecting gives it an edge in the global minerals race. Closer to home in India, Kerala's Chief Electoral Officer is rolling out AI-based tools to make election processes faster and more efficient, and YouTube is expanding its shopping affiliate program in India with new AI-powered tools, bringing Amazon, AJIO, and Meesho into the fold as shopping activity on the platform grows sharply. On the corporate governance side, Loyalty Juggernaut earned ISO/IEC 42001 certification for responsible AI management, and home products company Masco appointed its first Chief Technology and AI Officer, signaling how central AI has become even in traditionally non-tech sectors. Blue Cloud Softech also landed a $15.5 million AI infrastructure order from IBM Cloud, another sign of steady enterprise investment in AI buildout. And a note of perspective from Jefferies: despite all the global hype around AI, India's own growth story is arguably more about energy, electrification, renewables, and manufacturing investment than about artificial intelligence specifically.

In biotech and health research, there's a lot of movement. ADARx Pharmaceuticals priced an upsized IPO at $446 million, a strong signal of investor appetite for next-generation siRNA therapeutics. Prime Medicine shares jumped after the FDA cleared its investigational new drug application for a gene therapy targeting Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency. Earendil Labs, an AI-powered biotech, announced a research collaboration with Genentech to develop bispecific antibodies for oncology, another example of AI and drug discovery increasingly intertwining.
