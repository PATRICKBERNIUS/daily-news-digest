Good morning. Let's start with the big story rattling markets: Wall Street took a hard look at the cost of the AI boom and didn't love what it saw. Thursday's selloff hit Alphabet, Amazon, Intel, and Meta hard, as investors finally started pushing back on the eye-watering spending these companies are pouring into AI infrastructure and data centers. The message from the market was pretty blunt — earnings still matter, even in the AI gold rush. ServiceNow got caught in the crossfire too, which is a bit unfair given it actually beat expectations, with revenue up over 20 percent year-on-year and strong subscription growth. But investors weren't in a forgiving mood, and the stock took a hit on guidance concerns anyway. That disappointment rippled overseas, with European futures opening lower Friday as the tech-driven selloff pushed money toward safer havens. So if you're watching your portfolio, brace for a choppy end to the week in tech.

Away from the market turmoil, there's a lot happening on the AI adoption and policy side globally. The Philippines just launched a national AI masterplan, aiming to move past simply using AI tools and actually build its own AI infrastructure and services, leaning on its existing IT and business process outsourcing strength. Meanwhile, McKinsey data shows India has become one of the most AI-saturated shopping markets in the world, with 76 percent of consumers now using AI tools to search, compare, and buy products — putting India second globally on that front. India's also been busy diplomatically, holding talks with Armenia that touched on trade, science and technology, AI cooperation, and counterterrorism. And in the fintech world, a U.S.-India dialogue in Mumbai focused on a more practical AI use case: using predictive models to flag borrower financial stress before loans actually go bad.

On the cybersecurity front, AU Small Finance Bank in India is investigating a potential data breach after a hacking group calling itself ScarlettGroup claimed to have stolen customer KYC records, PAN and Aadhaar numbers, and internal corporate data from a Salesforce-hosted platform. It's part of what's been a rough month for data breaches more broadly.

Now to biotech and health, where there's a lot of activity. Aspen Neuroscience priced its IPO, raising roughly 140 million dollars to advance its autologous cell therapy for Parkinson's disease — a notable vote of confidence in regenerative medicine. Allogene Therapeutics announced it'll share preliminary safety and efficacy data in November from its ALLO-329 autoimmune disease trial, alongside its third-quarter results, with data also being presented at a major rheumatology conference. In Spain, a biotech called ALLOX closed a 10.5 million dollar seed round to expand its AI-powered drug discovery platform targeting historically hard-to-drug proteins, and brought on an experienced biotech executive to chair its board. There was also a flurry of smaller corporate news, including a new CFO appointment at the French biotech Enodia Therapeutics.

On the science side, there's a genuinely strange and fascinating finding out of Aarhus University: researchers discovered that bacteria can keep producing DNA even after they're dead, powered by dormant viruses called bacteriophages sitting inside the dead cells. Using synthetic DNA, scientists showed they could actually trigger these zombie-like cells to generate new genetic material, which could open doors in environmental biotech and bioenergy down the road.

Rounding out the policy and business landscape, India's Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal pushed states to streamline business regulations and urged the pharmaceutical industry to shift from generics toward higher-value, innovative drugs and biotech. In agriculture, MustGrow Biologics and Bayer are expanding their mustard-based biocontrol and soil health products across Europe, the Middle East, and Africa, while a separate piece out of Southeast Asia made the case that digital farming tools only matter if they actually reach smallholder farmers — arguing connectivity and affordability, not flashier tech, are the real bottleneck.

That's the rundown — markets nervous about AI spending, a wave of global AI adoption and policy news, and steady progress across biotech from cell therapy IPOs to some genuinely weird microbiology.
