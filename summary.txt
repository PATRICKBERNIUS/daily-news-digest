Good morning. Let's start with the big theme running through today's news: artificial intelligence and its real-world impact on jobs and economies across Asia. A new IMF report finds that AI is already cutting employer demand by nearly 10 percent in occupations most exposed to the technology, and this shift is happening fast, within just a few years of generative AI's launch. The core challenge for countries like India, Indonesia, and the Philippines is stark: these nations need to create enormous numbers of jobs for young, growing workforces, but AI is transforming the very sectors that were expected to absorb them. The consensus from multiple analyses today is that the outcome isn't predetermined by how fast AI advances, but by how well governments, businesses, and workers adapt through skills development and smart policy. On a related note, South Korea offers a interesting counterpoint, an IMF official there says the country's economy is actually being propped up by the AI investment boom, suggesting the technology cycle still has room to run despite global headwinds. Korean universities are also racing to expand AI degree programs as higher education scrambles to keep pace with industry demand.

But there's a note of caution too. A strategist at BCA Research warned today that this AI investment boom carries real risk: if productivity gains don't keep up with the massive capital being poured in, the world could see a significant market correction. That's worth watching given how much of global growth right now is being underwritten by AI-related spending.

On the corporate and product side, Sam Altman says OpenAI is now working on a humanoid robot, with a demo possibly as soon as 2027, as the company shifts focus beyond chatbots and into physical hardware. Hyundai is making a similar move, doubling down on robotics and what it calls "physical AI" as it tries to fend off Chinese competitors and reverse a slump in its stock price. Meanwhile, Anthropic has waded into the policy fight by pitching its own AI safety bill to US lawmakers, proposing that AI companies be legally required to test their models and publicly share the results, a sign that the industry itself is trying to shape regulation before governments do it for them. And in a lighter but telling cultural moment, South Korean reality TV is now exploring human-AI romance, with a show called "My AI Partner: Strange Love" featuring celebrities dating AI avatars, pool dates, sleepovers, and all, showing just how mainstream AI companionship has become as entertainment.

Switching to health and biotech, there's encouraging news on cancer care in India. The government has capped trade margins on 137 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs at 30 percent of the maximum retail price, a move expected to meaningfully lower costs for cancer patients. Separately, researchers at the Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Biotechnology in Thiruvananthapuram have received a 7 crore rupee grant to develop a biopsy-free, blood-based test for early cervical cancer detection, which could be a major step toward faster, less invasive diagnosis. In the US, the government is backing pharmaceutical supply security with an 80 million dollar loan to biotech company Antheia to boost domestic production of essential drug ingredients, part of a broader push to reduce reliance on foreign drug manufacturing.

On the business and markets front, foreign portfolio investors sold off a notable amount in Indian equities last week, though analysts say the market isn't under serious stress, with some buying returning toward the end of the week. Globally, smartphone shipments dropped sharply, down 22 percent year over year, as rising memory chip costs have pushed phone prices higher and squeezed the whole industry.

A couple of other items worth knowing about: there's a sobering piece today on the growing risk of a nuclear detonation in space, with a defense expert warning that Russia already has the technology to pull off a high-altitude nuclear event that wouldn't kill anyone directly but would be a massive escalation and disruption to satellites and global infrastructure. And on a warmer note, Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman met with survivors of the 2018 Tham Luang cave rescue in Bangkok, the dramatic rescue of the youth football team trapped in a flooded cave that captcaptured the world's attention years ago.

That's the roundup for today, plenty happening on the AI and biotech fronts, with the common thread being how fast these technologies are moving and how governments and industries are racing to keep up.
