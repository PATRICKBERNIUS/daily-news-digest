Good morning. Let's start with the story dominating markets right now: fears about an AI bubble are rattling investors across Asia and beyond. South Korea's Kospi index plunged as much as 9.3 percent, triggering circuit breakers that halted trading, as investors suddenly question whether the AI boom has staying power. That selloff bled into tech stocks more broadly, with the Nasdaq 100 closing down 2 percent and semiconductor stocks taking an even harder hit, falling 4.5 percent. The core tension here is familiar — inflation concerns are colliding with sky-high valuations in chip and AI-adjacent companies, and investors are getting nervous about whether the spending boom can keep justifying the prices.

That said, not everyone is pulling back. OpenAI is actually pointing to Europe as an underexploited opportunity, noting that AI agents now account for more than half the output generated through its enterprise platforms in the EU, and arguing there's a widening gap between adoption and the productivity gains businesses could actually capture. Meanwhile, law firm Freshfields is reportedly exploring spinning out its AI unit, Freshfields Lab, into a separate company that insiders think could be worth billions — a sign that even traditionally conservative industries see real commercial value in AI tools, bubble fears notwithstanding. On the geopolitical side, the Philippines has joined the US-led "Pax Silica" initiative, a pact aimed at securing supply chains in AI, semiconductors, and critical minerals while countering China's dominance in the AI race — though questions are already being raised about how much the Philippines actually stands to gain from it.

In corporate and government AI news, Mumbai's government, under IT and AI minister Ashish Shelar, kicked off the first phase of a major waterfront redevelopment project in Bandra with Godrej Properties, part of a broader push toward green, walkable urban spaces. In India more broadly, trade talks between India and the US are reportedly nearing finalization ahead of a visit from Secretary of State Rubio, with most major issues resolved. And Karnataka's IT and biotechnology minister Priyank Kharge announced plans for a structured framework to get Global Capability Centres working more closely with government on CSR initiatives.

On the consumer tech side, WhatsApp is rolling out a new "Guest Calling" feature globally that lets you invite people onto free, unlimited video or voice calls even if they don't have the app — a clear shot at Zoom and Teams. In India, phone users will soon see caller names automatically from November 1st, as telecom regulators mandate a new caller-ID system across major carriers to cut down on scam calls. And in the gadget world, Nothing is reportedly developing smart glasses to rival Meta's Ray-Bans, with one version focused on music and calls and another featuring a camera, ChatGPT integration, and translation tools. On the auto front, Chinese manufacturers Changan and Jetour both made moves at international motor shows, pushing multi-brand EV lineups and tech-forward "mobile third space" concepts for global markets.

Turning to biotech and health, there's a lot of movement in gene therapy and drug development. Ocugen finalized a licensing deal with Roots Pharmaceutical to bring its gene therapy for retinitis pigmentosa to the Middle East and North Africa, a deal that could be worth over 250 million dollars in milestones plus royalties. Vir Biotechnology signed on with Gilead to jointly develop T-cell engager therapies for solid tumors. Continuity Biosciences landed a 9.6 million dollar grant to develop a long-acting implant that could deliver HIV prevention medication for years from a single placement. And Siren Biotechnology bolstered its scientific advisory board with a former FDA oncology regulator as it pushes its cancer therapy toward clinical trials.

There's also an interesting convergence of AI and biotech showing up in several places. A Dutch company called polySCOUT, spun out of research institute TNO, is using AI to design custom polymers in weeks instead of years, dramatically speeding up materials development. INmune Bio is partnering with Quantum Computing Inc. to apply quantum optimization and AI to Alzheimer's drug research, marking the quantum company's first pharma client. And on a stranger note, a US biotech company says it's making real progress toward resurrecting the extinct dodo, claiming it's in the final stages of genome analysis.

Finally, on the health and wellbeing front, IIT Kharagpur distributed personalized wellbeing cards to over 16,500 students as part of a push to address student mental health following a string of student deaths — part of a broader conversation in India about mental health support on college campuses. And in Indian politics, the Congress party sharply criticized a new rural employment scheme, the VB-G RAM Act, arguing that its technology-driven implementation excludes vulnerable and tribal communities rather than helping them.

That's the rundown — the big throughline today is really AI's dual nature: massive market anxiety about a possible bubble on one hand, paired with steady, almost quiet expansion of AI into law, biotech, drug discovery, and everyday consumer tech on the other.
