Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, where trust and risk are dominating the conversation. Jamie Dimon, CEO of JPMorgan Chase, made it clear on an earnings call that the bank will not give external AI agents access to customer accounts, calling the technology too risky to let loose on sensitive financial data. That caution is echoed elsewhere in the finance world — Australia's corporate regulator is now quietly probing the collapsed stock market listing of AI data center company Firmus, while a separate Australian watchdog is warning that AI is fueling a wave of "big fakes," meaning increasingly convincing scams targeting consumers and investors. Over in Ireland, business leaders are voicing similar unease, with a new survey showing more than half now see AI as a cybersecurity threat rather than a business opportunity, and one in four saying they're actively nervous about sophisticated AI-driven attacks.

The geopolitical fight over AI is also intensifying. The US government has banned its agencies from using China's DeepSeek AI tools, citing national security, right as China fires back with its own draft restrictions on exporting 40 sensitive technologies, including AI, semiconductors, solar tech, and biomedicine. It's another sign that the AI race is increasingly becoming a trade and security battleground rather than just a commercial one. Meanwhile, on the infrastructure side, telecom players are pushing AI deeper into everyday networks: Jio's Akash Ambani laid out big bets on satellites, 6G, and AI-powered phone calls at India Mobile Congress, and Nokia and SoftBank announced a joint AI-RAN field trial planned for Japan in 2027, blending artificial intelligence directly into radio access networks.

In biotech and health, the big headline is a major acquisition: Germany's Merck KGaA is buying UK-based Silence Therapeutics in a $1.3 billion cash deal, sending Silence's shares surging. Elsewhere in biotech funding, a Berkeley startup called Onyx Bio, founded by Roger Chen and Amit Lakhani to build what they call a "virtual immune system," raised $8 million from investors including Dimension Capital. It's part of a broader wave of startup funding activity — Tenstorrent, the AI chipmaker, led the pack with a massive $1.5 billion Series E raise at a $6.2 billion valuation, alongside notable investments in biotech, healthcare, and enterprise software companies like Nouscom, Even Healthcare, and Vessla. In India, policymakers are also leaning into this space, with NITI Aayog's Abhay Karandikar saying the government's new RDI fund aims to spur roughly ten times more private investment, targeting AI, biotech, and space technology as priority sectors.

On the health research front, President Trump signed an executive order directing the federal government to expand research into women's health across all stages of life, a priority championed by First Lady Melania Trump. And in a lighter health-adjacent story, Ekta Vishnoi, a senior Indian government official in the Department of Biotechnology, won three gold medals at the World Masters Powerlifting Championships in Reno, lifting over 362 kilograms to beat competitors from Canada and Japan.

On the markets and corporate side, Australia's ASX dipped as a slump in BHP's copper business and a surprise announcement of 2,500 job cuts at NAB weighed on the index, even as tech stocks clawed back some early losses. In Taiwan, Micron is facing a labor standoff, with its Taoyuan union planning a strike this Thursday over a bonus and profit-sharing dispute. And in semiconductors, NTT's device subsidiary is leading a four-company research effort to develop advanced optical chiplet technology, another sign of how much international collaboration is going into next-generation chip infrastructure.

That's the big picture for today — AI's growing pains dominating finance and geopolitics, a flurry of biotech dealmaking and funding, and steady investment in telecom and semiconductor infrastructure shaping what comes next.
