Good morning. Let's start with AI, because it's dominating the headlines again today, both in the markets and in everyday life.

On the corporate side, we're seeing AI translate into real financial momentum. Palantir continues its remarkable run, with revenue jumping 96% in the third quarter, as the company successfully pivots from its government and defense roots toward booming demand from private sector commercial clients. Tempus AI is having a strong day too, up 11% on Wall Street after beating expectations, with its oncology diagnostics business up 32% and minimal residual disease testing volumes quadrupling, a sign that AI-driven molecular diagnostics is finally proving out its profitability case. Meanwhile, Meta is reportedly looking to raise at least $20 billion in the bond market, tapping Citigroup and Morgan Stanley to gauge investor interest, all to keep funding its enormous AI infrastructure buildout. And in retail, Zalando just launched its first AI shopping agent, a defensive move as the European fashion platform grapples with margins that came in below expectations and growing anxiety that AI could fundamentally disrupt how people shop online.

There's also a strong thread today about AI and the workforce. In India, a Michael Page survey found that 97% of professionals are eager to build AI skills and 88% expect it to boost productivity, even as a separate report shows Indian companies are granting AI systems more autonomy in business processes while still struggling to prove clear financial returns from those investments. On a more personal note, there are a couple of feel-good stories floating around: an engineering student landed a job offer worth roughly 50 lakh rupees a year without ever formally applying, simply because a product he built using AI caught a company's attention, and there's a profile of an IIT Kanpur graduate whose path went from making samosas and jalebis at his father's sweet shop in Lucknow to building an AI career in Dubai. Seoul is getting in on the talent push too, with around 100 AI, chip, biotech, and robotics companies gathering next week for a major job fair aimed at young job seekers. And UNESCO has teamed up with Google in Brazil to train digital creators on building more trustworthy, responsible AI-generated content, as concerns grow about misinformation online.

Shifting to health tech and biotech, there's an interesting device story out of China: BrainCo, which is positioning itself as a rival to Elon Musk's Neuralink, is developing a brain-computer interface aimed not at paralysis or communication, but at obesity, using brain stimulation to suppress appetite as a kind of electronic alternative to GLP-1 drugs. Closer to home in India, Eternal founder Deepinder Goyal is making waves with his new Temple wearable, claiming it measures heart rate more accurately than several popular competitors, including the Apple Watch, and it's now open for pre-booking.

On the biotech and pharma front, it's a fairly active day for clinical pipeline news. NorthSea Therapeutics will present late-breaking Phase 2 data on its drug orziloben for a rare liver disease at the big AASLD Liver Meeting in Denver this week. Okogen announced its first patient has been dosed in a Phase 1/2 trial for a new antiviral treatment targeting acute infectious conjunctivitis. Dianthus Therapeutics disclosed new equity inducement grants tied to recent hires, a fairly routine corporate filing. And Vir Biotechnology's CEO is set to present at the Jefferies Healthcare Conference in London later this month. In India, Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal met with biotech and biopharma startups to talk through regulatory easing and funding support, signaling continued government attention on growing that sector, and the Rajiv Gandhi Centre for Biotechnology has opened PhD applications for its 2027 session, with the deadline coming up on November 8th. There's also a thoughtful piece questioning India's new draft rules on reducing laboratory animal use, arguing the policy is out of step with the technology and institutional practices the country has actually adopted.

Finally, a few broader tech and infrastructure notes: India's data centre capacity is projected to grow five-fold by 2030, pulling in roughly 3 lakh crore rupees in investment as AI and cloud demand accelerates. Mumbai's BEST transport authority added 44 new air-conditioned electric buses across five routes, part of the city's ongoing push toward cleaner public transit. And in Germany, a startup called Eden Energy has built a commercial energy storage system out of 112 retired e-scooter batteries, an inventive approach to giving used batteries a second life while scaling toward larger storage capacity.

That's the roundup for today, plenty of momentum in AI earnings and adoption, some fascinating health-tech experiments, and steady progress across the biotech pipeline.
