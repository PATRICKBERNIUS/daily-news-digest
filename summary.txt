Good morning. Let's start with the biggest theme running through today's news: whether the AI boom is heading for trouble. Ray Dalio didn't mince words, calling AI a classic bubble nearing its bursting point, pointing to rising interest rates, mounting debt-fueled investment, and gains concentrated in just a handful of tech giants. That view got some institutional backing too — IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva warned in Singapore that the global economy is caught between a negative energy supply shock and a positive AI demand shock, a combination that could destabilize growth. And Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra weighed in as well, saying elevated AI asset valuations are a genuine risk to the global outlook, though he flagged something he sees as even bigger: the cyber risks that come with AI's rapid, widespread adoption. That concern is already playing out in financial markets — Hong Kong stocks fell today partly on selling in tech and biotech shares, alongside rising oil prices and soft economic data.

On the flip side of that risk conversation, there's a growing push toward using AI defensively. One piece lays out how cybersecurity is evolving into "agentic security," where AI systems don't just assist human analysts but actually detect and investigate threats on their own, reshaping security operations rather than just adding another tool to the stack. There's also a notable economic-equity angle: economist Daron Acemoglu is urging the industry to prioritize "pro-worker" AI design, arguing that the current trajectory of AI development risks widening inequality and accelerating job losses unless companies build tools that complement workers rather than simply replace them.

Meanwhile, there's a lot of momentum on the institutional and policy side of AI, especially out of India. Ahead of the India Mobile Congress, which Prime Minister Modi will inaugurate tomorrow, Communications Minister Jyotiraditya Scindia has made 6G, AI, domestic telecom manufacturing, and affordable digital access the core pillars of the country's tech strategy. In Karnataka, the state government signed a memorandum of understanding with the New York-based voice AI company ElevenLabs to explore applications like voice restoration, safety tools, and easier access to government services — part of a broader state push that also included cabinet approval of a 704-crore-rupee project to digitize the judiciary and a major reorganization of Bengaluru's city police into five commissionerates. On the research side, India and Switzerland agreed to deepen their science and technology partnership, with Switzerland committing an additional 45 million Swiss francs toward joint work spanning AI, biotechnology, quantum tech, space, and deep-ocean research. And in the U.S., Penn State announced new grants to help faculty integrate AI into teaching and learning.

In connectivity and infrastructure news, Starlink revealed it has built 20 satellite gateway sites across India, designed specifically to meet local data security rules, as part of its push to bring high-speed internet to underserved areas — and the company says its longer-term roadmap includes deeper integration of AI and computing into its satellite network. Airtel, meanwhile, is rolling out new postpaid plans starting at 499 rupees that include a free annual international roaming trip, along with data, calls, and entertainment perks.

On the defense and industrial front, Anduril and the U.S. Navy announced a massive 6.6-billion-dollar investment to rebuild America's submarine industrial base, including 3.7 billion dollars for a new software-defined shipyard that's expected to create more than three thousand jobs. In Poland, the state development bank BGK is launching a new 114-million-euro fund, called Vinci Space Tech, to back high-growth space technology companies. And in India, the government is touting progress in additive manufacturing — essentially industrial-scale 3D printing — as a way to make the country more competitive in advanced manufacturing, while a smaller manufacturer, Sharvaya Metals, inaugurated a new aluminum extrusion facility aimed at supplying the EV, aerospace, and defense sectors.

Finally, on the biotech side, it was a quieter day for scientific breakthroughs but a busy one for business moves. Clinical-stage biotech Remix Therapeutics, which works on RNA-targeted small molecule therapies, added Michael Landsittel to its board. In New Jersey, Portal Innovations opened a new biotech hub in New Brunswick offering lab space and infrastructure for health and life sciences startups. And a hybrid investor forum kicked off in New York this week, giving listed companies across biotech, digital health, mining, and energy a chance to present directly to investors.

That's the rundown — the dominant story today is really the tension between AI's economic promise and the mounting warnings, from Dalio to the IMF to central bankers, that the hype may have outrun the fundamentals.
