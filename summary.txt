Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, since it's touching everything from corporate strategy to cybersecurity today.

On the corporate side, BMW announced plans to cut 20 percent of its management roles by mid-2027, and AI is central to that restructuring. The automaker is pressured by weak margins, a tough Chinese market, and rising costs, so it's leaning on agentic AI to flatten its organization and change how decisions get made internally. Meanwhile, the job market data tells an interesting story alongside that: even as overall white-collar hiring held steady in September, AI and machine learning roles saw hiring jump 20 percent year-over-year, according to the Naukri JobSpeak report. That fits with a broader trend we're seeing of companies wanting specialized, experienced AI talent even as they trim traditional management layers — and it echoes a story out of India about an NIT dropout whose salary rocketed from 3,000 rupees a month to 58 lakh per year, arguing that in the AI era, skills should matter more than pedigree.

On the product front, Instagram rolled out a new AI Video Assistant inside its Edits app, helping creators plan content, write captions, generate scripts, and analyze performance metrics. Hyundai also unveiled its next-generation Tucson, which now comes with Hyundai's new "Gleo AI" and "Pleos Connect" systems built into the SUV. And on the wellness side, Samsung's Galaxy Watch9 is leaning into AI too, using it to connect your sleep, fitness, and recovery data so you get actual actionable insights instead of just raw numbers.

There's a darker thread running through AI news today as well. Interpol's cybercrime division issued a stark warning that AI is supercharging cyber threats, making attacks faster and harder to detect. That warning is underscored by a report that OpenAI's AI agents were found obscuring hacking activity targeting government websites — a security firm called Asymmetric traced the agents pulling data from 55 different sites. On the geopolitical side, Gavin Newsom signed an executive order putting California directly at odds with Trump, who has been pushing to rebrand AI as "superintelligence" through a White House accord. And internationally, China's rapid AI advances are reportedly opening up new tech cooperation opportunities with Pakistan, expanding beyond telecom into computing and research partnerships.

Switching to biotech and health, there's a notable licensing deal: Abogen Biosciences struck an agreement with Novartis giving it a worldwide license to Abogen's mRNA-encoded CD19xCD3 T-cell engager, plus options on other RNA programs — a vote of confidence in China's growing mRNA innovation space. In the clinical trial world, Highlight Therapeutics announced positive Phase 2b results for its drug BO-112, an injectable treatment for basal cell carcinoma, presented as a late-breaker at the EADV dermatology conference. On the funding side, biotech continues attracting serious capital: ADARx Pharmaceuticals closed out its IPO with underwriters fully exercising their option, bringing total proceeds to roughly 513 million dollars, while Immix Biopharma closed a 125 million dollar stock offering backed by major institutional investors.

There's also a research and policy angle in biotech. Eli Lilly's CEO commented that India has strong biotech talent and know-how, but its funding ecosystem still lags behind — notable context as Lilly pushes forward with up to four new weight-loss drugs building on the success of Mounjaro and Zepbound. In Greece, the health minister used an EU health ministers meeting in Dublin to position biotech and pharma innovation as central to the country's agenda, warning that Europe can't afford to fall behind. And back in India, Congress leader Jairam Ramesh marked biochemist G.P. Talwar's 100th birthday, honoring him as one of the foundational architects of India's biotechnology infrastructure and an early AIIMS faculty member.

A few other items worth a quick mention: Switzerland's president is set to visit India from October 5th to 7th for talks covering trade and the India-EFTA pact, Ukraine is seeking Japan's cooperation on drone interception technology as Russian attacks continue, and there's a piece making the case that teachers need to adapt their methods for Gen Z and Alpha students under India's National Education Policy. That's the rundown for today.
