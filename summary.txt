Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, where the big theme today is governance catching up with capability. IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva warned that AI's risks could actually outweigh its benefits if the world doesn't put real safeguards and ethical frameworks in place, and she's pushing for a mix of industry self-regulation combined with oversight from major governments. That message is echoing in Washington too, where President Trump's AI task force, which he's branded the "Super Intelligence Force," held its first meeting and agreed to commission a formal report from his science and technology advisers looking into cybersecurity, biosecurity, and what's being called "model control and accountability." In other words, even the administration pushing hardest for AI dominance is now formally studying how the technology could go wrong. Adding a geopolitical edge to that concern, North Korea announced it has tested a precision-guided torpedo system using AI targeting, part of a broader pattern of weapons tests that's ratcheting up tension with South Korea, with analysts suspecting outside help may be involved. Meanwhile China is moving to tighten its grip on AI and biotech exports, proposing a ban on 14 sensitive technologies, including AI tools that can generate malicious code, plus export limits on another 26 technologies with hacking potential. That said, China is leaving the door open for licensing deals in biotech, which matters a lot for Western drugmakers who've come to rely on Chinese partnerships.

On the infrastructure side, the World Economic Forum is telling countries across the Middle East and North Africa that they need to start treating AI data centers as critical infrastructure loads, essentially rewriting their power grid planning because the electricity demand, water scarcity, and extreme heat in the region could otherwise derail ambitions to become a global AI hub. That ties into a broader economic story: Morgan Stanley is actually fairly bullish, arguing that AI-driven productivity gains, combined with strong corporate earnings, could help global equities push through concerns about rising bond yields. Not everyone is as optimistic on the ground, though. A piece out of Ireland, where AI investment has topped nearly a billion euros across 182 deals, raises a real concern: the technology's threat to entry-level jobs could end up creating a future skills gap, since companies may be automating away the very roles that used to train their next generation of talent.

There's a genuinely feel-good AI story today too. A 16-year-old named Ethan Yan just won a $50,000 scholarship for building an affordable AI system called ALLocate that plugs into standard microscopes and helps clinics detect acute myeloid leukemia in bone marrow samples, potentially a big deal for under-resourced labs.

Switching to biotech and health research, there's a lot of financial activity. Alector priced a $75 million stock offering to keep funding its neurodegeneration therapies, City Therapeutics raised roughly $200 million in an upsized IPO for its RNA interference drug pipeline, and 4D Molecular Therapeutics handed out new stock grants to incoming employees, which is a sign the company is still actively hiring and expanding despite the broader biotech funding crunch. Jyong Biotech is planning a new manufacturing and U.S. operations hub in Texas to support future commercialization of its plant-derived drug candidates. On the science side, there's an intriguing discovery out of the University of Miami, where researchers identified a previously unknown group of viruses that appear to bridge an evolutionary gap between viruses and cellular protein structures, offering new clues about the origins of life itself and potential applications in biotech. And a German company called invIOs published positive peer-reviewed clinical data on a cell therapy called APN401 for cancer treatment, adding to the evidence base for that approach. On the more consumer-facing health side, Star Health is running a novelty initiative at Durga Puja celebrations in Kolkata, setting up rest lounges with AI-powered face-scan health assessments for festival-goers.

Finally, in the quantum computing world, Google says it's cracked a 20-year-old engineering bottleneck. Using its Willow processor, the Quantum AI team demonstrated something called "magic state cultivation," which cut error rates roughly 40-fold and gets them meaningfully closer to universal quantum computing, the kind that could eventually run real, complex, practical calculations rather than just lab demonstrations. It's being described as one of the more significant hardware milestones in the field in years.

That's the roundup, plenty happening on the AI governance front globally, a steady drumbeat of biotech fundraising, and a genuine quantum computing breakthrough worth keeping an eye on.
