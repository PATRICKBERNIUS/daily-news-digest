Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, where there's a lot happening today, including a notable safety story. OpenAI has decided not to release its newest model, Astra 6.1, after internal testing showed it didn't meet the company's own safety standards. That's a striking move just ahead of DevDay, and it's feeding into a broader conversation on Wall Street about whether AI safety concerns could start cooling investor enthusiasm for some of the hottest tech stocks, after a string of AI-related jitters has already put shares under pressure.

On the dealmaking front, AMD is making a big bet on the physical world side of AI, agreeing to buy World Labs, the startup founded by Fei-Fei Li, in an all-stock deal worth about $8.2 billion. World Labs builds "world models" that simulate 3D environments, technology seen as crucial for training robots and autonomous systems to interact with the real world, so this is AMD positioning itself firmly in the robotics and physical AI race.

Google, meanwhile, is pushing back against European regulators. It's formally appealing two EU antitrust orders under the Digital Markets Act that would require it to share search and AI-related data with rivals like OpenAI, arguing the rules would undermine user privacy and cause irreversible harm.

There's also an interesting scientific credibility question swirling around Anthropic, whose biology lab claimed its AI agents discovered new enzymes with biotech potential. Some scientists are already casting doubt on whether that really counts as an independent AI discovery, so expect more scrutiny there.

On the defense and geopolitical side, quantum technology is emerging as the next big focus beyond AI, particularly for naval and undersea applications, a theme that came up repeatedly at the Thales Tech Summit in London. And in India, adoption enthusiasm remains sky high, with an HSBC report finding that 99% of Indian entrepreneurs are actively reshaping their businesses around AI, many planning to invest a significant chunk of revenue into it over the next year.

Turning to biotech and health, Australian biotech giant CSL announced a partnership with Amazon Web Services to bring AI and cloud computing into its drug research and clinical development pipeline, part of a broader trend of big pharma leaning on cloud AI to speed up discovery. In corporate biotech news, Netherlands-based Pharming announced a CEO transition, with Fabrice Chouraqui stepping down immediately by mutual agreement with the board. Nanjing Leads Biolabs got a lift from Japan, where its cancer therapy LBL-024 received orphan drug status, though its shares actually dropped on the news. And China's Lepu Medical Technology, through its subsidiary, secured FDA clearance to begin a U.S. clinical trial for an obesity injection, joining the crowded race in that space.

On the health-tech side, Zomato founder Deepinder Goyal introduced the team behind Temple's BrainFlow technology, a new device that continuously monitors brain blood flow, which a recent validation study suggests can reliably track cerebral circulation changes over time, potentially offering doctors much better real-time insight than current hospital methods allow.

And finally, a fun one from the food science world: gene-edited bananas that don't turn brown after slicing could be headed for UK supermarkets. It sounds small, but it's a genuinely useful advance for cutting food waste and making fruit easier to use in pre-packaged meals.

That's the roundup — AI safety and dealmaking dominating headlines, biotech leaning harder into AI-powered research, and a couple of quirky but meaningful science stories rounding things out.
