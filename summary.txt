Good morning. Let's start with the biggest story in tech and AI today: Anthropic's bumpy road toward going public. A securities filing briefly circulated to banks suggested the Claude chatbot maker could raise as much as $69 billion in its IPO, but Anthropic pulled that filing back, saying the pricing details were inaccurate. Separately, the company has told regulators it's targeting a valuation of roughly $1.3 trillion, planning to sell 825 million shares. So the exact terms are still murky, but the scale of ambition here is staggering, and it's happening right as serious questions are swirling about whether AI spending has gotten ahead of itself.

That bubble question is showing up everywhere today. S&P Global Ratings is warning that the US-China AI race could hit real financial turbulence over the next three years, potentially including a bubble burst and mass layoffs, as the contest increasingly splits into China's state-directed approach versus America's market-driven one. Adding to the jitters, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has dropped out of the world's top ten richest people, with his net worth falling about $6.5 billion to $148 billion as Nvidia shares slid, reflecting broader weakness across the chip and tech sector. Huang is also dealing with a personal storm back in his native Taiwan, where reports of an alleged affair tied to a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy have stirred a mix of sympathy and disbelief among supporters who've long admired his role in Taiwan's tech rise.

Despite all that, not everyone on Wall Street is spooked. Morgan Stanley, UBS, and HSBC are telling clients last week's AI stock selloff looks like an overreaction rather than the start of a real correction, arguing that fears of tougher Democratic regulation probably won't be enough to slow down Big Tech's massive infrastructure spending. Meanwhile, a new GWI survey adds a cautionary note to all this AI enthusiasm: nearly six in ten senior business leaders admit they've made a major decision based on AI insights that turned out wrong, and 82% say they've paid a real price for AI errors, yet only about a third routinely double-check what the AI tells them.

On the business and funding side, AI money is still flowing fast despite the turbulence. Today's startup funding roundup shows investors pouring cash into next-gen infrastructure, defense manufacturing, and AI data centers, including Amsterdam's Onodrim Industries landing a $300 million Series A at a $1.3 billion valuation and Texas-based Mavria pulling in $130 million to build factory-made data centers. In gaming, Krafton, the company behind PUBG and BGMI, reported a 35.5% drop in quarterly operating profit despite sales growth, even as it preps a new winter-themed BGMI update for later this month.

Now to biotech and health, where there's a lot of clinical-stage progress. Tiziana Life Sciences shared encouraging follow-up data on its intranasal drug foralumab, showing consistency between positive Phase 2a trial results and longer-term benefits seen in patients with non-active secondary progressive MS. Lifordi Immunotherapeutics presented Phase 1 data on LFD-200, a subcutaneous antibody-drug conjugate aimed at autoimmune and inflammatory disorders. And ME Therapeutics, a Vancouver-based biotech working on immune-cell cancer therapies, announced it's bringing on an investor awareness firm to raise its profile. On the patent front, Ireland's Applied Biopharm Consulting filed its first patent for a muscle-targeting gene therapy technology aimed at treating Duchenne muscular dystrophy. And for investors watching the biotech space, analysts are flagging Telix Pharmaceuticals, Neuren Pharmaceuticals, and Amplia Therapeutics as buy-rated picks, with one seen as having potential for a 400% jump.

On the health policy side, there's a reminder that India remains a critical supplier of generic drugs to the US, underscoring just how difficult it's proving for America to bring pharmaceutical manufacturing back home despite ongoing self-reliance efforts.

Rounding out the business and local news: Indian tech firm Hind Rectifiers posted a 54% profit drop despite strong revenue growth, squeezed by rising costs, while foreign investor selling in Indian equities eased in October, with tech and telecom stocks drawing renewed interest. And in San Francisco, the Mission Branch Library reopens this Saturday after a $40 million renovation, bringing upgraded technology and restored architecture back to the neighborhood along with a free celebration party.

That's the briefing — plenty of momentum in AI funding and biotech trials today, but also growing signs that both investors and business leaders are starting to ask harder questions about where all this AI money is actually going.
