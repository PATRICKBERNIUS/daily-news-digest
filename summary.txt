Good morning. Let's start with artificial intelligence, which dominates today's headlines on several fronts. In a major industrial move, Abu Dhabi National Oil Company has become the first energy company in the world to deploy OpenAI's frontier technology, signing a landmark partnership with OpenAI and tech group G42 to integrate advanced AI models into its operational and commercial planning. This comes as ADNOC's CEO Sultan Al Jaber also noted that global oil reserves have shrunk by roughly a billion barrels due to the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the conflict around Iran, a reminder that geopolitical instability is still shaping energy markets even as companies push ahead with AI transformation.

Speaking of AI's trajectory, OpenAI's Sam Altman made waves by claiming advanced AI models are now mostly smarter than the brightest scientists and capable of handling large portions of white-collar work, a bold statement that's sure to fuel ongoing debates about AI's trajectory and its impact on jobs. Not everyone is racing in the same direction, though. Apple's new CEO John Ternus is taking a notably different approach than rivals, prioritizing careful, deliberate integration over the aggressive deployment of autonomous AI agents that companies like OpenAI are pushing.

On the academic and philosophical side, there's growing interest in AI's deeper implications. Notre Dame's John Behrens is presenting at Seton Hill University on AI's role in daily life and the common good, while Stanford researcher Dr. Ishan Shivanand has launched a white paper at IIT Ropar in India proposing a framework for defining and measuring "wisdom" in AI, drawing surprisingly on ancient texts like the Shiv Sutras. Together these signal a growing effort to ground AI development in ethical and philosophical frameworks, not just raw capability.

Markets are feeling the AI effect too. Indian equities, the Sensex and Nifty, rallied more than 1.6% on strong Q2 earnings, improving economic indicators, and foreign investors buying back in amid what's being called a global rotation in AI-related stocks. But not all the AI investment story is rosy. BlackRock's Ben Powell cautioned that after the recent sell-off in US tech stocks, this earnings season is raising the bar for the AI trade, with investors now demanding real evidence that massive AI spending is translating into actual earnings and cash flow. On a related note, investment firm Carmignac has launched a new UK-domiciled technology fund aiming to capture long-term opportunities from this wider tech and AI wave.

In health and biotech, there's a promising development out of research being presented at the American Heart Association's upcoming Scientific Sessions: combining AI analysis with wearable devices and blood pressure cuffs may be able to predict a dangerous hypertensive crisis up to three days before it happens, which could be a significant tool for managing high-risk patients. Meanwhile, in the broader biotech investment space, OrbiMed's Geoffrey Hsu is highlighting three smaller biotech companies he sees as having strong potential, noting that drug approvals have held up well despite turmoil at the FDA and looming patent cliffs for major pharmaceutical companies. And in India, Karnataka is making a major push to expand its innovation ecosystem beyond Bengaluru, with a 1,000 crore rupee LEAP initiative aiming to support 25,000 startups across electronics, IT, and biotechnology sectors.

Finally, a few things worth flagging on the tech and policy front. There's growing public debate around privacy, with one opinion piece arguing that the spread of Flock license-plate-reading cameras shows the US needs a constitutional amendment for information privacy, since legislation simply can't keep pace with surveillance technology. Separately, there's a push for Congress to do more to support parents on children's online safety. And on the enterprise side, companies rolling out AI assistants are being urged to think carefully about content security, since an AI tool is only as safe as the sensitive documents and data it can access. If you're in the market for a new TV, there's also a practical rundown comparing OLED, QLED, and Mini LED technology to help you figure out which offers the best picture for your money. And rounding out the economic picture, India's Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman emphasized that the country's economic resilience is rooted in its population's aspirations and the strength of its domestic market, a theme that ties back to the broader optimism we're seeing in Indian equities today.
