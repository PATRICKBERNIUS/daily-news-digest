Good morning. Let's start with the AI story that's dominating markets and boardrooms alike. Super Micro Computer just sharply raised its revenue and gross margin guidance for the quarter, citing red-hot demand for AI server infrastructure, a clear sign that the AI buildout is still accelerating rather than slowing down. That theme carries through to Anthropic, which is reportedly considering spending as much as 750 billion dollars on computing capacity as it gears up for an eventual IPO, an eye-watering figure that underscores just how capital-intensive the AI race has become. Meanwhile SpaceX is leaning into AI too, with Elon Musk projecting the company could hit a trillion dollars in revenue by 2028, and speculation swirling about a possible merger with Tesla, both factors keeping SpaceX shares very much in focus.

Not everyone is just riding the hype, though. McKinsey is out with a report warning that AI might actually be slowing down corporate decision-making rather than speeding it up, because executives are struggling to keep pace with the sheer volume of AI-generated analysis and verify the assumptions behind it. In other words, companies are generating insights faster than humans can responsibly sign off on them. There's a similar undercurrent in a new global survey on AI in education, which found that while the technology is speeding up students' work, a majority of Indian students worry it's coming at the cost of actually understanding material deeply. And a feature on Meta's new AI shopping agent, Muse, raises a related ethical question: as these agents hunt down bargains for consumers, the hidden cost may be squeezed wages and safety standards further down the supply chain.

On the corporate and policy side, AI's footprint keeps expanding. China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology is drafting new policies to push deeper integration of AI into manufacturing. More than 100 Japanese companies have set up or expanded AI-focused innovation hubs in India, moving well beyond old-school back-office outsourcing. And in India, Reliance's Jio Platforms had a blockbuster IPO day, its 3.2 billion dollar offering fully subscribed within two hours, with executives explicitly pointing to AI, cloud and enterprise services as the company's next growth engine. There's also a charming counter-narrative here, the 235-year-old Swiss watchmaker Girard-Perregaux is betting that the more AI takes over our lives, the more people will crave and value things made by hand.

Switching to biotech, it's a big day for dealmaking and drug development. Germany's Merck KGaA agreed to acquire UK-based Silence Therapeutics in a 1.3 billion dollar deal, sending Silence's shares sharply higher. In mRNA, ScionxBio struck a global licensing and collaboration deal with Sanofi to develop longer-lasting versions of Sanofi's mRNA candidates using Scion's extended-durability platform. There's also encouraging vaccine news: Patronus Biotech received ethical approval in Australia to begin first-in-human trials of three malaria vaccine candidates, while separately, researchers are highlighting progress on fridge-free vaccines, pointing to a tetanus-diphtheria shot that doesn't need refrigeration, which could be a real breakthrough for immunization in places with unreliable electricity and cold-chain infrastructure. In oncology, PDS Biotechnology bolstered its board with three pharma industry veterans as it pushes a colorectal cancer treatment toward Phase 3 trials, and YD Bio partnered with EG BioMed and digital health platform JoiiUp to roll out an AI-powered cancer risk assessment tool for corporate wellness programs, blending biotech with AI in a practical consumer application. On the regenerative medicine front, PolarityBio picked up a top research award for Phase 3 trial results on a new treatment for diabetic foot ulcers, and a Berkeley startup called Onyx Bio, founded by Roger Chen and Amit Lakhani, raised 8 million dollars to pursue the ambitious goal of building what they call a "virtual immune system."

Rounding out the broader tech and industry picture, Ashtead Technology, listed on London's AIM market, agreed to a 505 million pound takeover by New York private equity firm Ember Infrastructure. Toyota is looking at bringing hybrid technology down into smaller car segments in India, betting on continued growth in that market. And Samsung wrapped up its Solve for Tomorrow competition in India, handing out 2 crore rupees in incubation grants to four student teams with promising tech-driven solutions, a nice reminder that amid all the mega-deals and trillion-dollar ambitions, there's still plenty of grassroots innovation happening too.

That's the briefing for today.
