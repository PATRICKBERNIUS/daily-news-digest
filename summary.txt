Good morning. Let's start with the biggest theme running through today's news: a growing sense that the AI investment boom may be entering a new, more cautious phase. Jefferies is out with a notable call, suggesting the AI investment cycle may have peaked — a mid-cycle correction, as they put it. US tech giants have been ramping up capital spending, but concerns are mounting about financing and profitability, and that could weigh on broader US economic growth. Jefferies is advising investors to look beyond AI, specifically toward healthcare and biotech, and they've actually raised their allocation to India to 14 percent on the back of this. Interestingly, Jefferies' Christopher Wood also flagged gold as a potential beneficiary here — his argument is that AI spending has effectively been propping up the US economy, and if that money dries up, the Fed may be forced to change course in a way that favors gold prices. So keep an eye on gold as a sort of barometer for AI sentiment.

On the corporate side, Samsung Electronics is a clear winner of the AI chip supercycle right now — it's on track to become the first Korean company ever to hold more than $146 billion in cash assets, largely thanks to surging chip demand tied to AI infrastructure. Meanwhile, S&P Global has an interesting twist on the robotics and automation story: they expect AI to meaningfully boost industrial robotics growth over the long term, but say manufacturers themselves may actually benefit less than their customers do, since competitive pressure and rising equipment utilization will likely squeeze margins even as the technology improves.

There's also a recurring message today from EY, repeated across several reports, that simply bolting AI onto customer service operations isn't enough. Companies need a genuine reset — redesigning operating models, technology systems, and workforce capabilities — if they want AI to actually translate into better customer experience and measurable business results. It's a caution against treating AI as a plug-and-play fix rather than a structural change.

On the regulatory and social side, there's growing global unease about AI's effect on jobs. In the Philippines, a senator has filed a bill in the Senate specifically aimed at protecting Filipino workers from AI-driven layoffs, proposing new labor code provisions. And in a smaller but telling story, Google's AI search tool came under scrutiny after it declined to directly confirm who won the 2020 US presidential election, instead pointing users to federal records — raising fresh questions about how AI systems handle sensitive, factual-but-politically-charged queries.

On a lighter tech note, Priscilla Chan revealed that her 10-year-old daughter has taken up "vibe-coding" — building things with AI-assisted coding tools — though she's drawn some boundaries around what her daughter is allowed to build, a small window into how AI tools are trickling down to even the youngest users.

In biotech, the University of Virginia got a $6 million boost from philanthropists to launch the UVA Biotech Innovation Fund, which will support long-term biotech research and innovation programs through the university's Manning Institute — a reminder that even as AI dominates headlines, biotech funding is quietly accelerating too, something that aligns with Jefferies' advice to watch that sector closely.

A few other items worth a quick mention: in the Philippines, DOST-PAGASA launched new weather monitoring tools and a facility in Davao del Sur, and separately, DOST's bamboo research institute announced it'll host an International Bamboo Congress in 2027, reflecting rising global demand for bamboo products. In Scotland, a researcher is raising concerns that tidal energy — one of the lesser-developed renewable technologies — could have unintended effects on marine food webs, a caution worth noting as governments push harder on green energy expansion. And on the cybersecurity front, a cautionary tale out of Pune: a man lost nearly 3.5 lakh rupees after clicking a phishing link disguised as a traffic violation payment notice — a good reminder to stay wary of unsolicited payment links, even ones that look official.

That's the roundup — the central thread today is really that AI enthusiasm is colliding with real-world friction, whether that's investor caution, labor protections, operational reality checks, or basic trust issues, even as the technology keeps reshaping company balance sheets and everyday behavior.
