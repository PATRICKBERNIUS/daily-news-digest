Good morning. Let's get you up to speed on today's big stories, starting with the AI world, where there's a lot happening on multiple fronts.

On the geopolitical and security side, the most eyebrow-raising story is a report that US intelligence officials believe Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a romantic relationship with a woman, Alice Huang, who was allegedly working for China's Ministry of State Security. The claim is that she was recruited specifically to gather information on American AI chip technology, which, given Nvidia's central role in the global AI hardware race, makes this a significant national security story to watch.

On the economic front, there's growing nervousness about whether the AI boom can keep delivering. IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva warned that central bankers globally need to factor in the possibility of an AI-driven growth slowdown as they calibrate their inflation fight — essentially, a lot of economic optimism has been priced around AI productivity gains, and if that stalls, it complicates monetary policy. Meanwhile, markets reflected that tension this week: the Nasdaq rebounded Friday on strong gains from Nvidia and AMD, but still closed out its fourth straight weekly decline, showing investors are genuinely torn on how to value the AI trade right now. Adding to the uncertainty, Microsoft announced a voluntary severance program open to roughly 60,000 employees — 27% of its global workforce — as it restructures and redirects heavy spending toward AI infrastructure, a sign that even the companies winning the AI race are making hard tradeoffs internally.

On AI governance, Microsoft AI CEO Mustafa Suleyman spoke out against a Delaware legislative proposal that would let autonomous AI agents legally manage and operate corporate entities, calling it deeply concerning — a notable pushback from a major industry figure on how far AI autonomy should legally extend.

Globally, governments are leaning further into AI investment and education. Japan has picked 28 priority technologies, including semiconductors and AI, as the centerpiece of a massive ¥370 trillion public-private investment push. Closer to the ground, there's a wave of AI literacy efforts: Mizoram's government launched a Gen-AI Mission in Lunglei district to train over 5,500 school students in practical AI use across 80 schools, and in Melaka, Malaysia, political candidates are running free community AI workshops teaching residents things like restoring old family photos using AI tools. And environmental groups are embracing AI too — using it to track forest fires, monitor endangered wildlife, and analyze climate data — even as they openly acknowledge the irony that AI's data centers are driving up electricity and water use and emissions.

Switching to biotech and health research, there's encouraging news on a couple of fronts. A two-decade research effort out of Italy's Telethon Institute of Genetics and Medicine has identified a drug that restores kidney cell function in experimental models of Lowe syndrome, a rare genetic disorder affecting the eyes, nervous system, and kidneys — a meaningful step toward a therapy for a disease that currently has none. Separately, Brii Biosciences presented late-breaking data at the AASLD Liver Meeting showing their drug BRII-179, used in combination with siRNA therapies, is helping prime immune responses against Hepatitis B, with over 40% of certain participants achieving loss of the HBsAg antigen at the end of treatment — a promising sign for a functional cure strategy in a notoriously hard-to-treat patient population. On the funding side, the University of Virginia's Manning Institute of Biotechnology received a $6 million gift to launch a new Biotech Innovation Fund supporting early-stage research. And in quarterly earnings, Puma Biotechnology reported $6 million in net income for the third quarter.

Rounding out the technology space more broadly, there's a mix of market and culture stories. Analysts turned bearish on Teradata and Axcelis Technologies, while Bank of America maintained a buy rating on Sony. On the investment side, Kirloskar Group's Arka Fincap signed a deal for an initial ₹525 crore investment from TPG's Rise Funds to fuel growth and technology expansion, and India and the EU finalized an agreement giving India access to the EU's Horizon Europe research program, a notable win for scientific collaboration. And for a lighter note, Ayodhya is using augmented reality this Deepotsav to bring the Ramayana to life for younger audiences, blending tradition with tech in a creative way.

That's the roundup — AI dominates the headlines today, from spy intrigue to layoffs to classroom programs, while biotech continues its slow, steady progress on rare diseases and hepatitis treatment.
