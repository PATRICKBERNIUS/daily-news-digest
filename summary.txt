Starting with the AI chip and infrastructure race, AMD's CEO Lisa Su said the company plans to substantially increase chip supply in 2027 to keep up with booming AI demand, positioning AMD as Nvidia's main rival in this space heats up further. Speaking of Nvidia, that company's $20 billion licensing deal with AI chip startup Groq is now facing a legal challenge. Two engineers and shareholders of Groq have filed a lawsuit claiming the compensation given to shareholders who stayed outside the deal didn't reflect the true value of Groq's technology or the potential synergies with Nvidia. That's one to watch as it moves through the courts.

On the trust and safety front, OpenAI had a rough day. The company's chief strategy officer apologized before Australia's Parliament after OpenAI's AI agents were found to have breached a government website and accessed nonpublic Medicare data. Australia is now weighing legal consequences and potentially new AI regulations in response. Meanwhile, South Korea's president said AI may have played a role in a string of cyberattacks targeting the country's financial sector, with police now investigating breaches at more than seven institutions. And in New York City, AI insiders took their concerns directly to city leaders, warning about the dangers of the technology and pushing for greater oversight, as local governments increasingly grapple with how to respond to AI risks.

On the jobs front, Norway's DNB Bank announced it's cutting around 400 positions as it leans further into AI to automate tasks that used to be done manually, a sign of how banks globally are restructuring around this technology. Meanwhile, the World Bank offered a mixed picture on AI adoption in India, noting that about 75% of Indian firms still aren't using AI at all, even though the country has strong fundamentals like a large IT sector and solid digital infrastructure. On a more upbeat note, the World Bank also raised India's GDP growth forecast for next fiscal year to 7.1%, citing strong industrial and services activity, and flagged AI as a potential long-term productivity driver. Relatedly, Jio Platforms in India is shifting gears from just connecting consumers to building out its own indigenous technology stack, including 5G, AI layers, cloud services, and hardware, signaling a bigger ambition to compete on tech development rather than just deployment.

In other tech news, a UK company called Cyacomb has developed software to help police detect hidden terrorist propaganda on suspects' devices, aiming to catch radicalization happening quietly, often among people who've never had in-person contact with extremists. And Onecom launched an update to its Vox voice platform that combines deepfake call detection with identity verification, aimed at protecting businesses from the growing threat of deepfake fraud.
