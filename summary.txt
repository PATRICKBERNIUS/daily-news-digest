Good morning. Let's start with the markets, because that's where the biggest ripple effect is today. Bitcoin tumbled to its lowest level in two years, dropping over five percent to around $55,830, and the culprit according to multiple reports is growing anxiety about AI stock valuations. Investors are getting nervous that the massive run-up in AI-related equities has outpaced reality, and that nervousness is spilling into other risk assets like crypto. Adding to that theme, Barclays Private Bank's 2027 outlook says AI will still dominate investment strategy going forward, but it's flagging real risks: computing demand may outpace supply, which could choke scalability, rising debt financing costs could squeeze the economics of AI infrastructure buildouts, and investors should be cautious on government bonds given elevated yields and inflation concerns. So the picture emerging is one of a market still betting big on AI, but increasingly aware the bet is getting stretched.

That tension is playing out geopolitically too. The US has tightened export restrictions on advanced AI chips, specifically targeting five countries to choke off China's ability to access American chip technology through overseas data centers. Analysts at Jefferies warn this could scramble global semiconductor supply chains and ironically accelerate China's drive toward chip self-sufficiency. China, for its part, isn't sitting still — it's expanding its own export curbs on critical minerals, AI, biotech, and industrial equipment, using its leverage against the US, India, and Japan, according to a Diplomat report. So the chip war is escalating on both sides, and it's increasingly framed as a fight over who controls the building blocks of the AI era.

On the research side, there's a genuinely interesting story out of Anthropic: a mathematician working there claims to have cracked a long-standing "holy grail" math problem with help from the company's Claude AI model. This is the second time in recent months an AI company employee has claimed a major math breakthrough assisted by AI, which is fueling the broader narrative that these tools are starting to meaningfully contribute to frontier research, not just assist with grunt work.

India had a busy day on the AI and tech investment front. Google announced a $5 million funding initiative for IIT Kharagpur, aimed at boosting AI research in Indic languages, agriculture, and educational technology, plus upgrading AI tooling across the campus. Meanwhile, Bengaluru Tech Summit wrapped up with 24 venture capital firms pledging roughly Rs 1,050 crore toward deeptech startups spanning AI, biotech, robotics, semiconductors, drones, and cybersecurity — a strong signal of investor confidence in India's deeptech ecosystem. There's also a lighter story: the India International Trade Fair in Delhi has launched "Playverse@Mandapam," an experiential zone blending gaming, VR, and AI-powered transformations with Indian cultural heritage, built with Hungama as digital partner. And e-commerce platform Meesho put another $100,000 into its AI subsidiary, Meesho Networks, which is still pre-revenue and focused on commercializing AI tech.

On the infrastructure and jobs side, Himachal Pradesh's Chief Minister inaugurated a new Software Technology Parks of India center in Kangra, a roughly Rs 18 crore facility expected to generate around 400 new jobs in the region. And in Karnataka, CSR firm SoulAce signed an MoU with the state government to build a circularity ecosystem focused on digital inclusion and responsible e-waste reuse.

Now to biotech and health, where there's a mix of good and tough news. On the tough side, Capricor Therapeutics got a Complete Response Letter from the FDA for its Duchenne muscular dystrophy cell therapy, Deramiocel — regulators say the data doesn't yet establish sufficient evidence of effectiveness, which is a real setback for patients and families who'd been hopeful about this treatment. Elsewhere in biotech, it was a fairly routine corporate news day: Iovance Biotherapeutics and Avalo Therapeutics both announced inducement stock grants to new employees under Nasdaq rules, and Malaysia-based Alps Group announced it has regained compliance with Nasdaq's minimum bid price requirement, so it keeps its listing.

On a more hopeful note, India's Science Minister Jitendra Singh said the country is positioning itself as a global hub for research into diabetes, genetic disorders, and next-generation healthcare, pointing to more than a dozen diabetes-related projects currently underway through the Department of Biotechnology and CSIR.

A few other notable items worth a quick mention: Adani Defence successfully tested its indigenously developed Bagheera AT1 anti-tank guided missile at Pokhran after 18 months of development. India's merchandise exports are booming, up 44 percent to Africa and 21 percent to Asia in the first half of this fiscal year. And in Punjab, BJP leader HS Phoolka has written to Union Home Minister Amit Shah criticizing the state government for failing to scale up two low-cost agricultural technologies, Triple Zero and Mitter Seeders, that could help curb stubble burning — pointing out the stark gap compared to the 60,000-plus Super Seeders already in use.

That's the rundown — the big through-line today is AI's dual identity, as both the hottest investment theme on the planet and now a source of real anxiety about overvaluation, all while the US-China tech rivalry keeps tightening around chips and critical resources.
