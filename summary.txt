Good morning. Let's start with the dominant theme of the day: AI's impact on jobs, which is coming at this from multiple angles. IIT Madras's Centre for Responsible AI put out a widely-covered white paper arguing that we're asking the wrong question when we worry about AI "taking jobs" — the real story is task-level disruption, not wholesale job elimination. Their key finding is that entry-level positions in India are especially exposed, since AI is automating a lot of the routine work that junior employees traditionally cut their teeth on. The researchers aren't panicking, though — they're recommending that companies pair AI rollout with reskilling programs and clear internal pathways so young workers can move into higher-value responsibilities rather than getting stuck or squeezed out. This fits into a broader wave of commentary today about AI and human reasoning — one piece makes the philosophical case that AI is increasingly encroaching on creative and scientific reasoning itself, the very thing that's historically separated human work from automation. On a lighter note, singer Benny Dayal pushed back on this narrative from the arts side, insisting AI can't replicate the emotional core of music and that using it to compose would be an "insult to artistes" — a nice counterpoint to all the efficiency talk.

On the geopolitics of AI, there's a real tug-of-war playing out. China is positioning itself as a champion of open-source AI cooperation, pitching itself to the Global South as a partner that can help developing economies build local AI capability rather than depend on Western platforms. Meanwhile, India's government is leaning into multilateral forums — the PMO highlighted Lok Sabha Speaker Om Birla's piece on the upcoming BRICS Parliamentary Forum, which will specifically tackle AI governance alongside institutional resilience and sustainability. And on the corporate side, Google's Sundar Pichai said in a podcast interview that Google Cloud is deliberately sacrificing short-term revenue, redirecting compute resources to back frontier AI development instead — a signal of just how much the big players are willing to spend to stay ahead.

In Indian tech and investment news, momentum looks strong. Glance, the InMobi-backed content platform, landed a strategic investment from L Catterton, which is betting on AI reshaping how people discover and shop for products. India's immersive tech market — think AR and VR — is projected to hit $7.8 billion by 2030, growing nearly 30% a year and outpacing the rest of Asia-Pacific. Office real estate demand held up well in the third quarter too, with banking and financial services firms and flexible workspace providers picking up the slack as IT sector demand cooled. And consumer and retail investment in India came in strong at $1.92 billion across 127 deals for the quarter, according to Grant Thornton. Zooming out globally, equity funds pulled in a hefty $28 billion in weekly inflows as easing bond yields and solid earnings boosted investor sentiment, with Asian markets and tech funds getting a good share of that money.

A few other notable tech stories: the US, UK, and Australia have launched a new joint project to develop underwater drones meant to protect undersea cables, which carry the vast majority of global internet traffic — this is really an extension of the broader US-China tech rivalry, just moving to the ocean floor now. In clean energy, India's INA Solar signed a five-year deal with California-based Caelux to build next-generation perovskite-tandem solar panels, aiming for close to 29% efficiency by 2028. And on the consumer gadget front, Zomato co-founder Deepinder Goyal is launching something called Temple — a wearable designed to monitor blood flow to the brain, pre-orders are opening soon if you're curious about pricing.

Switching to biotech and health, there's a steady drumbeat of clinical and corporate news. Danish biotech Genmab is partnering with Incyte on an early-stage trial for a combination treatment targeting mutated metastatic colorectal cancer. Oruka Therapeutics is set to report Week 16 data today from its Phase 2b trial of ORKA-001, a biologic for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis — worth watching if you follow the skin-disease treatment space. On the corporate governance side, Australian biotech giant CSL has named Gordon Naylor, who'd been serving as interim CEO, as its new permanent chair starting in 2027. And Indian company Symbiotec Pharmalab got a bullish "Buy" call from Motilal Oswal, with analysts pointing to its shift from a basic steroid API manufacturer into a broader fermentation and biologics platform, targeting what they see as a $50 billion addressable market. Finally, in the Philippines, public servants are undergoing a pilot training program in agricultural biotechnology, aimed at helping local government officials better understand and govern biotech applications in farming at the community level.
